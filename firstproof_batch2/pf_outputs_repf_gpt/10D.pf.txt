<THEOREM_STATEMENT id="1">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(\Gamma\) be a finite simplicial graph with \(|\mathcal V(\Gamma)|\geq 3\).
- For each \(v\in \mathcal V(\Gamma)\), let \((M_v,\varphi_v)\) be a separable tracial von Neumann algebra.
- Assume that \(\Gamma\) is irreducible, equivalently its complement graph \(\Gamma^c\) is connected.
- Assume that each \(M_v\) has a trace zero unitary.
- Let
\[
M_\Gamma=\overline{\bigotimes}_\Gamma M_v
\]
be the graph product von Neumann algebra.

Statement :
\[
M_\Gamma
\]
is properly proximal.
</THEOREM_STATEMENT>

Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.

The graph product von Neumann algebra \(M_\Gamma\) is properly proximal.

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="1.2">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(N\) be a tracial von Neumann algebra containing a trace zero unitary.

Statement :
\(\dim N\geq 2\). Moreover, if \(A\) and \(B\) are tracial von Neumann algebras such that one factor has dimension at least \(2\) and the other has dimension at least \(3\), then the free product \(A\ast B\) is properly proximal.
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="1.2">
A trace zero unitary is non-scalar, hence the ambient von Neumann algebra has linear dimension at least \(2\). The free product assertion is exactly the foundational dimension criterion for proper proximality of free products.

External citation used: Boutonnet, Ioana, and Peterson (2021), Theorem 4.1.
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="1.3">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(\Gamma\) be an irreducible graph on vertices \(1,2,3\).
- Assume \(\Gamma^c=K_3\).
- Assume each \(M_i\) has a trace zero unitary.
- Let \(M_\Gamma=\overline{\bigotimes}_\Gamma M_i\).

Statement :
\(M_\Gamma\) is properly proximal.
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="1.3">
The graph \(\Gamma\) is totally disconnected. Thus,
\[
M_\Gamma=M_1\ast M_2\ast M_3.
\]
Rewrite this via associativity as the free product of \(A=M_1\) and \(B=M_2\ast M_3\). Since \(\dim M_2\geq 2\) and \(\dim M_3\geq 2\), their free product contains a diffuse subalgebra, isomorphic to the group von Neumann algebra of \(\mathbb Z_2\ast \mathbb Z_2\), yielding infinite linear dimension:
\[
\dim B=\infty\geq 3.
\]
Also \(\dim A\geq 2\). Hence \(A\ast B\) satisfies the threshold in Lemma 1.2 and is properly proximal.
</LEMMA_PROOF>

Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.

- Assume \(\Gamma^c=P_3\), with complement edges \((1,2)\) and \((2,3)\).
- Assume each \(M_i\) has a trace zero unitary.
- Let \(M_\Gamma=\overline{\bigotimes}_\Gamma M_i\).

Statement :
\(M_\Gamma\) is properly proximal.
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="1.4">
The graph \(\Gamma\) strictly contains only the single edge \((1,3)\). Thus,
\[
M_\Gamma=(M_1\overline{\otimes}M_3)\ast M_2.

\[
A=M_1\overline{\otimes}M_3,\qquad B=M_2.

The tensor product yields
\[
\dim A=\dim M_1\times \dim M_3\geq 2\times 2=4\geq 3,
\]
and \(\dim B\geq 2\). By the symmetric form of the free product criterion in Lemma 1.2, \(A\ast B\) is properly proximal.
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="2">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(n\geq 4\).
- Let \(\Gamma\) be an irreducible finite simplicial graph with \(|\mathcal V(\Gamma)|=n\).
- For each \(z\in \mathcal V(\Gamma)\), let \((M_z,\varphi_z)\) be a separable tracial von Neumann algebra with a trace zero unitary.
- Let \(G=\Gamma^c\).
- Assume the induction hypothesis: for every irreducible graph \(\Delta\) with \(3\leq |\mathcal V(\Delta)|<n\), and every family of separable tracial vertex algebras with trace zero unitaries, the graph product von Neumann algebra \(M_\Delta\) is properly proximal.

There exists a vertex \(v\in \mathcal V(\Gamma)\) such that, writing
\[
\Gamma'=\Gamma\setminus\{v\},\qquad A=M_{\Gamma'},

the graph \(\Gamma'\) is irreducible, \(A\) is properly proximal, and if
\[
L=\operatorname{lk}_\Gamma(v),\qquad C=M_L,\qquad B=C\overline{\otimes}M_v,

then \(L\subsetneq \mathcal V(\Gamma')\) and
\[
M_\Gamma=A\ast_C B.

\[
(\Gamma\setminus\{v\})^c=G\setminus\{v\},

the graph \(\Gamma'=\Gamma\setminus\{v\}\) remains irreducible and has \(n-1\geq 3\) vertices.

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="3">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(n\geq 4\).
- Let \(\Gamma\) be irreducible with \(|\mathcal V(\Gamma)|=n\), and let \(G=\Gamma^c\).
- Let \(v\in \mathcal V(\Gamma)\) be a non-cut vertex of \(G\).

- Let \(Q_B\) be the orthogonal projection onto the closed span of all reduced alternating words whose leftmost letter belongs to \(B\ominus C\).
- Let \(\varphi\) be an \(M_\Gamma\)-central state on \(\mathbb B(L^2M_\Gamma)\) extending the trace \(\tau_{M_\Gamma}\).

where \(x,y\in M_\Gamma\) and \(D\subset M_\Gamma\) is an amenable von Neumann subalgebra.

\[
\varphi(Q_A)=0,\qquad \varphi(Q_B)=0,\qquad \varphi(e_C)=1,\qquad \varphi(e_A)=1.

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="3.1">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Work in the setting of Proposition 3.
- Let \(Q_A,Q_B,e_C\) be as defined there.

Statement :
The reduced alternating word decomposition of \(L^2M_\Gamma\) gives the exact orthogonal equality
\[
1=e_C+Q_A+Q_B.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="3.1">
The Hilbert space \(L^2M_\Gamma\) decomposes into the orthogonal direct sum of \(L^2C\) and the spaces of reduced alternating words over the orthocomplements \(A\ominus C\) and \(B\ominus C\). The summand whose leftmost letter lies in \(A\ominus C\) is the range of \(Q_A\), and the summand whose leftmost letter lies in \(B\ominus C\) is the range of \(Q_B\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="3.2">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Work in the setting of Proposition 3.
- Let
\[
u=1\otimes u_v\in C\overline{\otimes}M_v=B.

Statement :
\[
u\in B\ominus C,\qquad uQ_Au^*\leq Q_B,\qquad \varphi(Q_A)\leq \varphi(Q_B).
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="3.2">
Since \(\tau(u_v)=0\), we have \(E_C(u)=0\), ensuring \(u\in B\ominus C\). By the alternating word decomposition in Lemma 3.1, left multiplication of any alternating word starting with \(A\ominus C\) by \(u\) strictly increases the word length by \(1\), producing a valid alternating word beginning with \(u\in B\ominus C\). Thus
\[
u\,\operatorname{ran}(Q_A)\subset \operatorname{ran}(Q_B),
\]
and since \(u\) is unitary,
\[
uQ_Au^*\leq Q_B.
\]
Applying the \(M_\Gamma\)-central state \(\varphi\),
\[
\varphi(Q_A)=\varphi(uQ_Au^*)\leq \varphi(Q_B).
\]
</LEMMA_PROOF>

w_2^*w_1=u_x^*u_y^*u_x^*u_x=u_x^*u_y^*.

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="3.4">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Work in the setting of Proposition 3.
- Let \(w_1,w_2\in A\ominus C\) be unitaries satisfying
\[
E_C(w_2^*w_1)=0.
\]

Statement :
The projections
\[
w_1Q_Bw_1^*,\qquad w_2Q_Bw_2^*
\]
are mutually orthogonal subprojections of \(Q_A\), and
\[
2\varphi(Q_B)\leq \varphi(Q_A).
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="3.4">
Using the alternating word decomposition from Lemma 3.1, for any vector in \(\operatorname{ran}(Q_B)\), left multiplication by \(w_i\in A\ominus C\) strictly increases the alternating word length by \(1\), mapping it to an alternating word starting with \(A\ominus C\). Thus
\[
w_iQ_Bw_i^*\leq Q_A,\qquad i=1,2.
\]
Moreover, for \(\xi,\zeta\in \operatorname{ran}(Q_B)\),
\[
\langle w_1\xi,w_2\zeta\rangle
=\tau(\zeta^*w_2^*w_1\xi)=0.
\]
This holds because \(E_C(w_2^*w_1)=0\), so the middle product \(w_2^*w_1\in A\ominus C\), and \(\zeta^*(w_2^*w_1)\xi\) remains a valid alternating word of length at least \(3\), which necessarily has trace zero. Hence the two projections are mutually orthogonal subprojections of \(Q_A\).

Applying the \(M_\Gamma\)-central state \(\varphi\),
\[
\varphi(w_iQ_Bw_i^*)=\varphi(Q_B),\qquad i=1,2.
\]
Therefore
\[
2\varphi(Q_B)
=
\varphi(w_1Q_Bw_1^*)+\varphi(w_2Q_Bw_2^*)
\leq \varphi(Q_A).
\]
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="3.5">

2\varphi(Q_B)\leq \varphi(Q_A)\leq \varphi(Q_B).

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="4.2">

- Let \(M\) be a tracial von Neumann algebra and \(A\subset M\) a von Neumann subalgebra.
- Let \(e_A\) be the orthogonal projection from \(L^2M\) onto \(L^2A\).
- Let \(\varphi\) be an \(M\)-central state on \(\mathbb B(L^2M)\) extending \(\tau_M\).
- Assume
\[
\varphi(e_A)=1.
\]
- Define a functional \(\psi\) on \(\mathbb B(L^2A)\), using the spatial identification
\[
\mathbb B(L^2A)\cong e_A\mathbb B(L^2M)e_A,
\]
by
\[
\psi(T)=\varphi(e_ATe_A).
\]

Statement :
\(\psi\) is an \(A\)-central state on \(\mathbb B(L^2A)\) extending the trace \(\tau_A\).
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="4.2">
Because \(\varphi(e_A)=1\), \(\psi\) is a valid state on \(\mathbb B(L^2A)\). For any \(a\in A\), \(a\) commutes with \(e_A\), and therefore
\[
\psi(aT)
=\varphi(e_AaTe_A)
=\varphi(ae_ATe_A)
=\varphi(e_ATe_Aa)
=\psi(Ta).
\]
Thus \(\psi\) is strictly \(A\)-central.

Moreover, since \(\varphi(e_A)=1\), we have \(\varphi(1-e_A)=0\). The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for states gives
\[
|\varphi(a(1-e_A))|^2
\leq \varphi(aa^*)\varphi(1-e_A)
=0.
\]
Hence, for \(a\in A\),
\[
\psi(a)
=\varphi(e_Aae_A)
=\varphi(ae_A)
=\varphi(a)
=\tau_M(a)
=\tau_A(a).
\]
So \(\psi\) extends the standard trace on \(A\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="4.3">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(M\) be a tracial von Neumann algebra and \(A\subset M\) a von Neumann subalgebra.
- Let \(e_A\) be the orthogonal projection from \(L^2M\) onto \(L^2A\).
- Let \(\varphi\) be a state on \(\mathbb B(L^2M)\) that vanishes on \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(M)\).
- Assume \(\varphi(e_A)=1\).
- Define
\[
\psi(T)=\varphi(e_ATe_A),\qquad T\in \mathbb B(L^2A).
\]
- Let \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(A)\) be the norm-closed ideal in \(\mathbb B(L^2A)\) generated by operators
\[
X e_D^A Y,
\]
where \(X,Y\in A\) and \(D\subset A\) is an amenable von Neumann subalgebra.

Statement :
\[
\psi\big|_{\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(A)}=0.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="4.3">
For a generator \(X e_D^A Y\), with \(X,Y\in A\) and \(D\subset A\) amenable, the spatial identification identifies \(e_D^A\) with the restriction of \(e_D^M\). On \(L^2M\), it acts as
\[
e_D^M e_A=e_Ae_D^Me_A.
\]
Therefore
\[
\psi(Xe_D^AY)
=\varphi(e_AXe_D^MYe_A).
\]
Since \(X,Y\in A\), the projection \(e_A\) commutes with \(X\) and \(Y\), which act by left multiplication. Hence the argument rewrites as
\[
Xe_Ae_D^Me_AY=Xe_D^MY.
\]
Thus
\[
\psi(Xe_D^AY)=\varphi(Xe_D^MY).
\]
Because \(D\subset A\subset M\) is an amenable subalgebra of \(M\) and \(X,Y\in A\subset M\), the operator \(Xe_D^MY\) belongs to \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(M)\) by definition. Since \(\varphi\) vanishes on \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(M)\),
\[
\varphi(Xe_D^MY)=0.
\]
By linearity and norm-continuity, \(\psi\) vanishes on all of \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(A)\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="4">
Assume for contradiction that \(M_\Gamma\) is not properly proximal. By Lemma 4.1, there exists an \(M_\Gamma\)-central state \(\varphi\) on \(\mathbb B(L^2M_\Gamma)\) extending \(\tau_{M_\Gamma}\) and vanishing on \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(M_\Gamma)\). Proposition 3 applies to this state and gives
\[
\varphi(e_A)=1.
\]
Define
\[
\psi(T)=\varphi(e_ATe_A),\qquad T\in \mathbb B(L^2A).
\]
By Lemma 4.2, \(\psi\) is an \(A\)-central state extending \(\tau_A\). By Lemma 4.3, \(\psi\) vanishes on \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(A)\). Since \(A\) is properly proximal, Lemma 4.1 prohibits the existence of exactly such a state. This contradiction shows that \(M_\Gamma\) is properly proximal.
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>

<THEOREM_PROOF id="1">
Proceed by mathematical induction on
\[
n=|\mathcal V(\Gamma)|.
\]
For \(n=3\), Proposition 1 proves the result.

Now let \(n\geq 4\), and assume the theorem holds for all irreducible graphs with \(k\) vertices, where \(3\leq k<n\). Proposition 2 supplies a non-cut vertex \(v\) of \(G=\Gamma^c\), an irreducible graph
\[
\Gamma'=\Gamma\setminus\{v\},
\]
a properly proximal subalgebra
\[
A=M_{\Gamma'},
\]
and the amalgamated free product decomposition
\[
M_\Gamma=A\ast_C B,\qquad C=M_{\operatorname{lk}_\Gamma(v)},\qquad B=C\overline{\otimes}M_v.
\]
Since the hypotheses of Proposition 4 are then satisfied, Proposition 4 gives that \(M_\Gamma\) is properly proximal. This completes the induction.
</THEOREM_PROOF>

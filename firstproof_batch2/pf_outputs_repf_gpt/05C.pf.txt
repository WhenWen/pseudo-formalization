<THEOREM_STATEMENT id="1">
Original label: Theorem \(\ref{thm:unique-invariant}\).

Context / abstract preserved from the paper:
- We prove that the Markov semigroup associated with the Dirichlet stochastic heat equation with drift \(\delta_0(u)\) has at most one invariant probability measure.
- In fact, under the hypotheses stated in the question, the invariant measure is unique and is the Gibbs perturbation of the Brownian bridge measure
\[
        \mu(\dd u)=Z^{-1}\exp\!\left(2\int_0^1 \1_{\{u(\xi)>0\}}\dd \xi\right)\gamma(\dd u),
\]
where \(\gamma\) is the law of the standard Brownian bridge on \([0,1]\).
- The proof uses three inputs: the reversible Gibbs structure of smooth approximations, the absolute-continuity theorem of Bounebache--Zambotti for the associated Dirichlet-form resolvent, and irreducibility of the weighted Ornstein--Uhlenbeck Dirichlet form.

Notation / definitions:
- Let
\[
        E:=C_0([0,1]),\qquad H:=L^2(0,1).
\]
We regard \(E\) as a Borel subset of \(H\).
- Let \(\gamma\) denote the law on \(E\) of the standard Brownian bridge from \(0\) to \(0\) over \([0,1]\). Equivalently, \(\gamma\) is the centered Gaussian measure on \(H\) with covariance \((-\Delta_D)^{-1}\), where \(\Delta_D\) is the Dirichlet Laplacian on \((0,1)\).
- Let
\[
        \mathsf H(r):=\1_{\{r>0\}},\qquad
        \Psi(u):=\int_0^1 \mathsf H(u(\xi))\,\dd \xi,\qquad u\in E.
\]
- Define
\[
        \mu(\dd u)
        := Z^{-1}e^{2\Psi(u)}\,\gamma(\dd u),
        \qquad
        Z:=\int_E e^{2\Psi(u)}\,\gamma(\dd u).
\]

Statement:
The semigroup \((P_t)_{t\geq0}\) has exactly one invariant probability measure on
\(E=C_0([0,1])\). It is the measure \(\mu\) defined above. In particular,
\(P_t\) has at most one invariant probability measure.

Bibliography preserved from the paper:
- S.~Athreya, O.~Butkovsky, K.~L\^e and L.~Mytnik, \emph{Well-posedness of stochastic heat equation with distributional drift and skew stochastic heat equation}, Comm. Pure Appl. Math. \(\mathbf{77}\) (2024), no.~5, 2708--2777.
- S.~K.~Bounebache and L.~Zambotti, \emph{A skew stochastic heat equation}, J. Theoret. Probab. \(\mathbf{27}\) (2014), no.~1, 168--201.
- M.~Fukushima, Y.~Oshima and M.~Takeda, \emph{Dirichlet Forms and Symmetric Markov Processes}, 2nd revised and extended edition, de Gruyter, Berlin, 2011.
</THEOREM_STATEMENT>

        := Z^{-1}e^{2\Psi(u)}\,\gamma(\dd u),
        \qquad

\[
        G_{1/n}(r)=\sqrt{\frac{n}{2\pi}}\,e^{-nr^2/2},\qquad
        B_n(r):=\int_{-\infty}^r G_{1/n}(a)\,\dd a,
\]

\[
        \Psi_n(u):=\int_0^1 B_n(u(\xi))\,\dd \xi,\qquad
        \mu_n(\dd u):=Z_n^{-1}e^{2\Psi_n(u)}\,\gamma(\dd u).
\]
Then \(B_n'=G_{1/n}\).

Statement:
Since \(0\leq \Psi\leq 1\), the measures \(\mu\) and \(\gamma\) are equivalent and their Radon--Nikodym derivative is bounded above and below by positive constants.
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="1">
Since \(0\leq \Psi\leq 1\), one has \(1\leq e^{2\Psi}\leq e^2\). Hence \(Z\) is finite and positive, and
\[
        \frac{\dd\mu}{\dd\gamma}=Z^{-1}e^{2\Psi}
\]
is bounded above and below by positive constants. Therefore \(\mu\) and \(\gamma\) are equivalent.
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="3">
Original label: Lemma \(\ref{lem:approx-reversible}\).

Section: Reversibility of the limiting semigroup.

Assumptions / definitions:
- Let \(P_t^n\) be the Markov semigroup for the regularized equation
\[
    \partial_t u^n=\frac12\partial_{xx}u^n+G_{1/n}(u^n)+\dot W
\]
with Dirichlet boundary conditions.
- This is standard for gradient-type stochastic heat equations with smooth drift.
- Let
\[
        \mu_n(\dd u)=Z_n^{-1}e^{2\Psi_n(u)}\,\gamma(\dd u).
\]

Statement:
For each \(n\), \(\mu_n\) is invariant and reversible for \(P_t^n\):
\[
        \int_E f\,P_t^n g\,\dd\mu_n
        =
        \int_E g\,P_t^n f\,\dd\mu_n
\]
for all bounded Borel \(f,g:E\to\R\) and all \(t\geq0\).
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="3.1">
Assumptions / definitions:
- Let \(\varphi,\psi\) be cylinder functions on \(H\).
- Let \(L_n\) be the generator of the regularized equation.
- Let \(L_0\) be the Ornstein--Uhlenbeck generator associated with the linear Dirichlet stochastic heat equation.

Statement:
The generator of the regularized equation is
\[
        L_n\varphi=L_0\varphi+\langle D\Psi_n,D\varphi\rangle_H,
\]
and, since \(\mu_n=Z_n^{-1}e^{2\Psi_n}\gamma\), the Gaussian integration-by-parts formula gives
\[
        \int \psi\,L_n\varphi\,\dd\mu_n
        =
        -\frac12\int \langle D\varphi,D\psi\rangle_H\,\dd\mu_n
        =
        \int \varphi\,L_n\psi\,\dd\mu_n .
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="3.1">
This is the Gibbs integration-by-parts computation for the smooth potential \(\Psi_n\): the drift term is \(\langle D\Psi_n,D\varphi\rangle_H\), and the density \(e^{2\Psi_n}\) exactly cancels it in the Gaussian integration-by-parts formula, yielding
\[
        \int \psi\,L_n\varphi\,\dd\mu_n
        =
        -\frac12\int \langle D\varphi,D\psi\rangle_H\,\dd\mu_n
        =
        \int \varphi\,L_n\psi\,\dd\mu_n .
\]
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="3.1"></DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="3.2">
Assumptions / definitions:
- Work in the setting of Proposition 3.
- Assume the cylinder-function symmetry identity of Lemma 3.1.

Statement:
The closure of \(L_n\) is self-adjoint in \(L^2(\mu_n)\), and the associated Markov semigroup is symmetric. Taking \(\psi\equiv1\) gives invariance of \(\mu_n\).
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="3.2">
By Lemma 3.1, \(L_n\) is symmetric on cylinder functions in \(L^2(\mu_n)\). Thus the closure of \(L_n\) is self-adjoint in \(L^2(\mu_n)\), and the associated Markov semigroup is symmetric. Taking \(\psi\equiv1\) gives invariance. This is the usual Gibbs-reversibility computation for stochastic quantization; see, for example, Bounebache--Zambotti [BZ14, Section 4] for the same calculation in the present Dirichlet setting.

External citations used: S.~K.~Bounebache and L.~Zambotti, \emph{A skew stochastic heat equation}, J. Theoret. Probab. \(\mathbf{27}\) (2014), no.~1, 168--201, Section 4.
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="3">
Lemma 3.2 gives symmetry of the Markov semigroup associated with \(L_n\) in \(L^2(\mu_n)\) and invariance of \(\mu_n\). Therefore, for all bounded Borel \(f,g:E\to\R\) and all \(t\geq0\),

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="4">
Original label: Lemma \(\ref{lem:limit-reversible}\).

Assumptions / definitions:
- Let \(P_t\) be the limiting semigroup for the singular equation.
- Let
\[
        \mu(\dd u)=Z^{-1}e^{2\Psi(u)}\,\gamma(\dd u).
\]

Statement:
The measure \(\mu\) is invariant and reversible for \(P_t\). Namely, for all bounded Borel \(f,g:E\to\R\) and all \(t\geq0\),
\[
        \int_E f\,P_tg\,\dd\mu
        =
        \int_E g\,P_tf\,\dd\mu .
\]
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="4.1">
Assumptions / definitions:
- Let \(f,g\in C_b(E)\).
- Fix \(t>0\).
- Assume the weak convergence of the regularized solutions:
\[
        P_t^n h(u)\longrightarrow P_t h(u),\qquad u\in E,
\]
for every \(h\in C_b(E)\).

Statement:
For bounded continuous \(f,g\),
\[
        \int_E f\,P_tg\,\dd\mu
        =
        \int_E g\,P_tf\,\dd\mu .
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="4.1">
By the assumed weak convergence of the regularized solutions,
\[
        P_t^n h(u)\longrightarrow P_t h(u),\qquad u\in E,
\]
for every \(h\in C_b(E)\) and every fixed \(t>0\). Therefore, using Proposition 2,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\int fP_t^ng\,\dd\mu_n-\int fP_tg\,\dd\mu\right|
&\leq
\|f\|_\infty\|g\|_\infty\,\|\mu_n-\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}        \\
&\quad
+\int |f(u)|\,|P_t^ng(u)-P_tg(u)|\,\mu(\dd u)
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Similarly,
\[
        \int gP_t^nf\,\dd\mu_n\longrightarrow \int gP_tf\,\dd\mu .
\]
The reversibility identity for \(P_t^n\) from Proposition 3 therefore passes to the limit and yields the displayed detailed-balance identity for bounded continuous \(f,g\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="4.1">2, 3</DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="4.2">
Assumptions / definitions:

- Assume the detailed-balance identity

        \int_E f\,P_tg\,\dd\mu
        =
        \int_E g\,P_tf\,\dd\mu

Since \(E\) is Polish and \(\mu\) is a probability measure, a monotone-class argument extends the detailed-balance identity to all bounded Borel \(f,g\). Taking \(g\equiv1\) gives invariance of \(\mu\).

<LEMMA_PROOF id="4.2">
Since \(E\) is Polish and \(\mu\) is a probability measure, a monotone-class argument extends the detailed-balance identity from bounded continuous functions to all bounded Borel \(f,g\). Taking \(g\equiv1\) gives invariance of \(\mu\).

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="5">
Original label: Lemma \(\ref{lem:dirichlet-form}\).

Section: The Dirichlet form and its irreducibility.

Assumptions / definitions:
- Let \(\mathcal{FC}_b^1\) denote the smooth bounded cylinder functions on \(H\).
- Define on \(\mathcal{FC}_b^1\) the bilinear form
\[
        \cE(F,G)
        :=
        \frac12\int_E \langle DF(u),DG(u)\rangle_H\,\mu(\dd u).
\]
- Because \(\dd\mu/\dd\gamma\) is bounded above and below by positive constants, this form is closable in \(L^2(\mu)\). Denote its closure by \((\cE,\cF)\).
- This proposition supplies the irreducibility input for the weighted Ornstein--Uhlenbeck Dirichlet form.

Statement:
The symmetric semigroup \((P_t)_{t\geq0}\) is the Markov semigroup associated with
\((\cE,\cF)\). Moreover, \((\cE,\cF)\) satisfies a Poincare inequality: there exists
\(C<\infty\) such that for every \(F\in\cF\),
\[
        \Var_\mu(F)\leq C\,\cE(F,F).
\]
Consequently, if \(F\in L^2(\mu)\) and \(P_tF=F\) in \(L^2(\mu)\) for all \(t\geq0\), then
\(F\) is \(\mu\)-a.s. constant.
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="5.1">
Assumptions / definitions:
- Work with the bilinear form
\[
        \cE(F,G)
        =
        \frac12\int_E \langle DF(u),DG(u)\rangle_H\,\mu(\dd u)
\]
on \(\mathcal{FC}_b^1\).
- The density \(\dd\mu/\dd\gamma\) is bounded above and below by positive constants.

Statement:
The form \(\cE\) is closable in \(L^2(\mu)\), and its closure is denoted by \((\cE,\cF)\).
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="5.1">
By Proposition 1, \(\dd\mu/\dd\gamma\) is bounded above and below by positive constants. Thus closability transfers from the Brownian-bridge Ornstein--Uhlenbeck gradient form on \(L^2(\gamma)\) to the weighted form on \(L^2(\mu)\). We denote the closure by \((\cE,\cF)\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="5.2">
Assumptions / definitions:
- Work with \(f=-2\mathsf H\).
- Let
\[
        \nu_f(\dd u)=\frac1{Z_f}\exp\!\left(-\int_0^1 f(u(\xi))\,\dd\xi\right)\gamma(\dd u).
\]
- Let \((\cE,\cF)\) be the closed form defined by
\[
        \cE(F,G)=\frac12\int_E \langle DF(u),DG(u)\rangle_H\,\mu(\dd u).

Statement:
The symmetric semigroup \((P_t)_{t\geq0}\) is the Markov semigroup associated with \((\cE,\cF)\).

<LEMMA_PROOF id="5.2">
For \(f=-2\mathsf H\), Bounebache--Zambotti [BZ14, Propositions 2.4 and 3.1] construct the quasi-regular Dirichlet form
\[
        \frac12\int \langle DF,DG\rangle\,\dd\nu_f,
        \qquad
        \nu_f(\dd u)=\frac1{Z_f}\exp\!\left(-\int_0^1 f(u(\xi))\,\dd\xi\right)\gamma(\dd u).
\]
Since \(f=-2\mathsf H\), we have \(\nu_f=\mu\). For smooth \(f\), their local-time formulation is equivalent, by the occupation-time formula, to
\[
        \partial_t u=\frac12\partial_{xx}u-\frac12 f'(u)+\dot W.
\]
For \(f=-2\mathsf H\), one has \(-\frac12 f'=\delta_0\) in the sense of distributions. Thus the Bounebache--Zambotti process solves the same singular SPDE. By the assumed uniqueness in law of the ABLM solution, the associated semigroup agrees with \(P_t\). Hence \(P_t\) is the semigroup of \((\cE,\cF)\).

External citations used: S.~K.~Bounebache and L.~Zambotti, \emph{A skew stochastic heat equation}, J. Theoret. Probab. \(\mathbf{27}\) (2014), no.~1, 168--201, Propositions 2.4 and 3.1. Named attribution preserved from the author text: the assumed uniqueness in law of the ABLM solution.
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="6">
Original label: Lemma \(\ref{lem:resolvent-ac}\), “Resolvent absolute continuity”.

Assumptions / definitions:
- For \(\alpha>0\), write the normalized resolvent as
\[
        \mathcal R_\alpha(x,A)
        :=
        \alpha\int_0^\infty e^{-\alpha t}P_t(x,A)\,\dd t,
        \qquad x\in E,\ A\in\cB(E).
\]

Statement:
For every \(\alpha>0\) and every \(x\in E\),
\[
        \mathcal R_\alpha(x,\cdot)\ll \mu.
\]
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="6">
Bounebache--Zambotti prove the absolute-continuity condition for the resolvent of the Dirichlet-form process associated with \(\nu_f\) for every bounded function \(f\) of bounded variation; see [BZ14, Proposition 2.5]. Applying their result to \(f=-2\mathsf H\) gives absolute continuity with respect to \(\mu\). Their Proposition 3.1 identifies the associated process as a weak solution of the corresponding local-time SPDE. By Lemma 5.2, for \(f=-2\mathsf H\) this is precisely the SPDE with drift \(\delta_0(u)\). The assumed uniqueness in law of the ABLM mild solution therefore identifies this resolvent with the resolvent of \(P_t\). Hence
\[
        \mathcal R_\alpha(x,\cdot)\ll\mu
\]
for every \(\alpha>0\) and every \(x\in E\).

External citations used: S.~K.~Bounebache and L.~Zambotti, \emph{A skew stochastic heat equation}, J. Theoret. Probab. \(\mathbf{27}\) (2014), no.~1, 168--201, Propositions 2.5 and 3.1. Named attribution preserved from the author text: the assumed uniqueness in law of the ABLM mild solution.
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="7">
Section: Uniqueness of the invariant measure.

Assumptions / definitions:
- Let \(\lambda\) be any invariant probability measure for \(P_t\) on \(E\).
- Let \(\mathcal R_\alpha\) be the normalized resolvent
\[
        \mathcal R_\alpha(x,A)
        =
        \alpha\int_0^\infty e^{-\alpha t}P_t(x,A)\,\dd t.
\]

Statement:
\[
        \lambda\ll\mu.
\]
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="7">
Let \(A\in\cB(E)\) satisfy \(\mu(A)=0\). By Proposition 6,
\(\mathcal R_\alpha(x,A)=0\) for every \(x\in E\). Since \(\lambda\) is invariant,
\[
\begin{aligned}
        \lambda(A)
        &=
        \alpha\int_0^\infty e^{-\alpha t}\lambda P_t(A)\,\dd t        \\
        &=
        \int_E \mathcal R_\alpha(x,A)\,\lambda(\dd x)
        =0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(\lambda\ll\mu\).
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>
<DEPS id="7">6</DEPS>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="8">
Section: Uniqueness of the invariant measure.

Assumptions / definitions:
- Let \(\lambda\) be an invariant probability measure for \(P_t\).
- Assume \(\lambda\ll\mu\).
- Write
\[
        h:=\frac{\dd\lambda}{\dd\mu}.
\]
Then \(h\geq0\) and \(\int h\,\dd\mu=1\).

Statement:
\[
        \lambda=\mu.
\]
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="8.1">
Assumptions / definitions:
- Let \(\lambda\) be invariant for \(P_t\).
- Let \(h=\dd\lambda/\dd\mu\).
- Assume \(P_t\) is symmetric with respect to \(\mu\).

Statement:
For every \(t\geq0\),
\[
        P_t h=h
\]
in \(L^1(\mu)\).
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="8.1">
For every bounded Borel \(\varphi\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
        \int_E \varphi\,P_t h\,\dd\mu
        &=
        \int_E P_t\varphi\, h\,\dd\mu                         \\
        &=
        \int_E P_t\varphi\,\dd\lambda                         \\
        &=
        \int_E \varphi\,\dd\lambda                            \\
        &=
        \int_E \varphi\,h\,\dd\mu .
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the first equality uses the symmetry of \(P_t\) in \(L^2(\mu)\), extended by duality to \(L^1(\mu)\)--\(L^\infty(\mu)\), from Proposition 4, and the third equality uses invariance of \(\lambda\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="8.1">4</DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="8.2">
Assumptions / definitions:
- Let \(N>0\).
- Set
\[
        h_N:=h\wedge N.
\]
- Assume \(P_t h=h\) in \(L^1(\mu)\) for every \(t\geq0\).

Statement:
For every \(t\geq0\),
\[
        P_t h_N=h_N
\]
\(\mu\)-a.s.
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="8.2">
Since \(r\mapsto r\wedge N\) is concave and nondecreasing, Jensen's inequality for the Markov operator \(P_t\) gives
\[
        P_t h_N \leq (P_t h)\wedge N=h_N,
\]
where Lemma 8.1 gives \(P_t h=h\). Integrating with respect to the invariant measure \(\mu\) from Proposition 4,
\[
        \int P_t h_N\,\dd\mu=\int h_N\,\dd\mu.
\]
Therefore \(P_t h_N=h_N\) \(\mu\)-a.s. for every \(t\geq0\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="8.2">8.1, 4</DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="8.3">
Assumptions / definitions:
- Let \(N>0\).
- Let \(h_N=h\wedge N\).
- Assume \(P_t h_N=h_N\) \(\mu\)-a.s. for every \(t\geq0\).

Statement:
For every \(N>0\), \(h_N\) is \(\mu\)-a.s. constant.
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="8.3">
Since \(h_N\in L^2(\mu)\), Lemma 8.2 gives \(P_t h_N=h_N\) in \(L^2(\mu)\) for every \(t\geq0\). Proposition 5 implies that \(h_N\) is \(\mu\)-a.s. constant.
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="8.3">8.2, 5</DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="8.4">
Assumptions / definitions:
- Let \(h=\dd\lambda/\dd\mu\), with \(h\geq0\) and \(\int h\,\dd\mu=1\).
- Assume that for every \(N>0\), \(h\wedge N\) is \(\mu\)-a.s. constant.

Statement:
\[
        h=1
\]
\(\mu\)-a.s.
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="8.4">
Taking \(N=2\), we obtain \(h\wedge2=c\) for some constant \(c\). Since
\[
        c=\int (h\wedge2)\,\dd\mu\leq \int h\,\dd\mu=1<2,
\]
the identity \(h\wedge2=c\) forces \(h=c\) \(\mu\)-a.s. Finally,
\(\int h\,\dd\mu=1\) gives \(c=1\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="8.4">8.3</DEPS>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="8">
By Lemma 8.4, \(h=1\) \(\mu\)-a.s. Since \(h=\dd\lambda/\dd\mu\), this gives \(\lambda=\mu\).
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>
<DEPS id="8">8.4</DEPS>

<THEOREM_PROOF id="1">
By Proposition 4, \(\mu\) is invariant. Let \(\lambda\) be any invariant probability measure for \(P_t\). Proposition 7 gives \(\lambda\ll\mu\), and Proposition 8 then gives \(\lambda=\mu\). Thus \(\mu\) is the unique invariant probability measure.

Remark preserved from the paper: The key point excluding additional singular invariant measures is the pointwise absolute continuity of the normalized resolvent in Proposition 6, corresponding to the original Lemma \(\ref{lem:resolvent-ac}\). Symmetry from Proposition 4 and the Poincare inequality from Proposition 5 alone imply uniqueness only among invariant probability measures that are already absolutely continuous with respect to \(\mu\).
</THEOREM_PROOF>

<THEOREM_STATEMENT id="1">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- A Bernoulli\((p)\) random variable is a random variable taking value \(1\) with probability \(p\) and value \(0\) with probability \(1-p\).
- For \(p\in[0,1]\), let \(\mathcal P(p)\) denote the assertion that for every integer \(m\ge1\), every choice of nonnegative real numbers \(w_1,\ldots,w_m\ge0\) with \(\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_i=1\), and every family \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\) of i.i.d. Bernoulli\((p)\) random variables, one has
\[
\Pr\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_i v_i\ge p\right]\ge p.
\]

Statement :
The assertion \(\mathcal P(p)\) holds exactly for
\[
p\in[0,\tfrac13]\cup\{\tfrac12,1\}.
\]
Equivalently, the complete set of probabilities \(p\) for which
\[
\Pr\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{m} w_i v_i\ge p\right]\ge p
\]
holds for every finite convex weight vector \((w_1,\ldots,w_m)\) and every i.i.d. Bernoulli\((p)\) family \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\) is
\[
\boxed{[0,\tfrac13]\cup\{\tfrac12,1\}}.
\]
</THEOREM_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="1">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(X_1,X_2,\ldots\) be i.i.d. real-valued random variables.
- For \(t\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
L(t):=\inf_{N\ge1}
\Pr\!\left[\frac{X_1+\cdots+X_N}{N}\ge t\right].
\]
- Let \(m\ge1\) and let \(w_1,\ldots,w_m\ge0\) satisfy \(\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_i=1\).

Statement :
One has
\[
\Pr\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{m} w_iX_i\ge t\right]\ge L(t).
\]
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="1.1">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(X_1,X_2,\ldots\) be i.i.d. real-valued random variables.
- Let \(m\ge1\) and \(w_1,\ldots,w_m\ge0\) satisfy \(\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_i=1\).

Statement :
For every real \(u\),
\[
\Pr\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_iX_i>u\right]
\ge
\inf_{N\ge1}
\Pr\!\left[\frac{X_1+\cdots+X_N}{N}>u\right].
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="1.1">
This is the one-dimensional half-line form of Hoeffding's extremal theorem for convex combinations of i.i.d. random variables.

External citations: W. Hoeffding, \emph{The extrema of the expected value of a function of independent random variables}, Annals of Mathematical Statistics \textbf{26} (1955), 268--275.
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="1.2">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Use the setting of Proposition 1.
- Fix \(t\in\mathbb R\).

Statement :
For every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\Pr\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_iX_i>t-\varepsilon\right]\ge L(t).

</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="1.2">

\[
\left\{\frac{X_1+\cdots+X_N}{N}\ge t\right\}
\subseteq
\left\{\frac{X_1+\cdots+X_N}{N}>t-\varepsilon\right\},
\]
and therefore
\[
\inf_{N\ge1}
\Pr\!\left[\frac{X_1+\cdots+X_N}{N}>t-\varepsilon\right]
\ge
\inf_{N\ge1}
\Pr\!\left[\frac{X_1+\cdots+X_N}{N}\ge t\right]
=L(t).
\]
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="1.2">1.1</DEPS>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="1">
By Lemma 1.2,
\[
\Pr\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_iX_i>t-\varepsilon\right]\ge L(t)
\qquad\text{for every }\varepsilon>0.
\]
As \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), the events
\[
\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_iX_i>t-\varepsilon\right\}

decrease to
\[
\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_iX_i\ge t\right\}.
\]
By continuity of probability from above,
\[
\Pr\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_iX_i\ge t\right]\ge L(t).
\]
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="2">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(X_{N,p}\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N,p)\).

Statement :
The following hold.

1. If \(0\le p\le \frac13\), then for every \(N\ge1\),
\[
\Pr[X_{N,p}\ge Np]\ge p.
\]
Equivalently, since \(X_{N,p}\) is integer-valued,
\[
\Pr[X_{N,p}\ge \lceil Np\rceil]\ge p.
\]

2. If \(p=\frac12\), then for every \(N\ge1\),
\[
\Pr[X_{N,1/2}\ge N/2]\ge \frac12.
\]

Moreover, in the first assertion equality occurs for \(N=1\) and \(0<p\le1/3\).
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

\Pr[X_{N,0}\ge0]=1\ge0.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="2.1">

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="2.2">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(0<p\le1/3\).
- Let \(X_{N,p}\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N,p)\).

Statement :
For every \(N\ge1\),
\[
\Pr[X_{N,p}\ge Np]
=
\Pr[X_{N,p}\ge \lceil Np\rceil]
\ge p.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="2.2">
For \(0<p\le1/3\), this is the classical binomial mean-tail estimate of Samuels and Jogdeo--Samuels. In the form needed here, that estimate states exactly that for every \(N\ge1\),
\[
\Pr[X_{N,p}\ge \lceil Np\rceil]\ge p.
\]
Because \(X_{N,p}\) is integer-valued, the events
\[
\{X_{N,p}\ge Np\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\{X_{N,p}\ge \lceil Np\rceil\}
\]
are identical.

External citations: S. M. Samuels, \emph{On the number of successes in independent trials}, Annals of Mathematical Statistics \textbf{36} (1965), 1272--1278; K. Jogdeo and S. M. Samuels, \emph{Monotone convergence of binomial probabilities and a generalization of Ramanujan's equation}, Annals of Mathematical Statistics \textbf{39} (1968), 1191--1195.
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="2.3">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(X_{N,1/2}\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N,1/2)\).

Statement :
For every \(N\ge1\),
\[
\Pr[X_{N,1/2}\ge N/2]\ge \frac12.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="2.3">
For \(p=1/2\), the binomial distribution is symmetric:
\[
\Pr[X_{N,1/2}=j]=\Pr[X_{N,1/2}=N-j]
\qquad (0\le j\le N).
\]
If \(N=2r+1\) is odd, then the values \(0,\ldots,r\) pair with the values \(2r+1,\ldots,r+1\), and exactly one member of each pair is at least \(N/2=r+\frac12\). Hence
\[
\Pr[X_{N,1/2}\ge N/2]=\frac12.
\]
If \(N=2r\) is even, then the values \(0,\ldots,r-1\) pair with \(2r,\ldots,r+1\), while \(r\) is the central value and is included in the event \(\{X_{N,1/2}\ge r\}\). Therefore
\[
\Pr[X_{N,1/2}\ge N/2]
=
\frac12\bigl(1-\Pr[X_{N,1/2}=r]\bigr)
+\Pr[X_{N,1/2}=r]
=
\frac12+\frac12\Pr[X_{N,1/2}=r]
\ge\frac12.
\]
</LEMMA_PROOF>

\Pr[X_{N,p}\ge Np].

</LEMMA_PROOF>

- \(\mathcal P(1)\) denotes the assertion that for every \(m\ge1\), every convex weight vector \(w_1,\ldots,w_m\), and every i.i.d. Bernoulli\((1)\) family \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\),

occurs only when \(N=2\), that is, when both \(v_1\) and \(v_2\) equal \(1\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>

Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Use the setting of Lemma 7.1.

Statement :

\Pr\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{2}w_i v_i\ge p\right]=p^2.

<LEMMA_PROOF id="7.2">
By Lemma 7.1,
\[
\Pr\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{2}w_i v_i\ge p\right]
=
\Pr[v_1=1,\ v_2=1].
\]
By independence,
\[
\Pr[v_1=1,\ v_2=1]=p^2.
\]
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="7">
Fix \(p\in(1/2,1)\). Lemmas 7.2 and 7.3 give, for the displayed choice of weights and Bernoulli variables,
\[
\Pr\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{2}w_i v_i\ge p\right]<p.
\]
Thus this choice of weights gives a counterexample. Therefore \(\mathcal P(p)\) fails.
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>
<DEPS id="7">7.2, 7.3</DEPS>

<THEOREM_PROOF id="1">
Proposition 3 proves that \(\mathcal P(p)\) holds for every
\[
p\in[0,\tfrac13].
\]
Proposition 4 proves that \(\mathcal P(1/2)\) holds, and Proposition 5 proves that \(\mathcal P(1)\) holds. Hence
\[
\mathcal P(p)\quad\text{holds for every}\quad
p\in[0,\tfrac13]\cup\{\tfrac12,1\}.
\]

It remains to show that no other probability works. Since \(p\) is a probability, \(p\in[0,1]\). The complement of
\[
[0,\tfrac13]\cup\{\tfrac12,1\}
\]
inside \([0,1]\) is
\[
\left(\frac13,\frac12\right)\cup\left(\frac12,1\right).
\]
For every \(p\in(1/3,1/2)\), Proposition 6 gives an explicit choice of weights for which the desired inequality fails. For every \(p\in(1/2,1)\), Proposition 7 gives an explicit choice of weights for which the desired inequality fails.

Therefore \(\mathcal P(p)\) holds exactly for
\[
p\in[0,\tfrac13]\cup\{\tfrac12,1\}.
\]
This proves the theorem.
</THEOREM_PROOF>

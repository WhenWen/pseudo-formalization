<THEOREM_STATEMENT id="1">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let
\[
E:=C_0([0,1])=\{v\in C([0,1],\mathbb R):v(0)=v(1)=0\},
\qquad H:=L^2(0,1),
\]
with \(E\) equipped with the supremum norm.
- Let
\[
e_k(x):=\sqrt2\sin(k\pi x),\qquad \lambda_k:=\pi^2k^2,\qquad k\ge1,
\]
be the Dirichlet eigenbasis of \(-\partial_{xx}\).
- Let \(\gamma\) denote Brownian-bridge law on \(E\), equivalently the centered Gaussian measure with covariance \((-\partial_{xx})^{-1}\):
\[
\mathbb E_\gamma[(v,e_k)_H(v,e_\ell)_H]=\frac{\delta_{k\ell}}{\lambda_k}.
\]
- The SPDE is
\[
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,t)
=
\frac12\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2}(x,t)
+\delta_0(u(x,t))+\dot W(x,t),
\qquad t\ge0,
\]
with \(u(x,0)=u_0(x)\in E\) and \(u(0,t)=u(1,t)=0\).
- Assumption ABLM-type input on the bounded interval: for every \(u_0\in E\) and \(T>0\), there is a unique-in-law adapted mild solution \(u\in C([0,T],E)\), and the solutions define a Markov semigroup \((P_t)_{t\ge0}\) on \(E\). For
\[
b_n(r):=G_{1/n}(r)=\sqrt{\frac n{2\pi}}e^{-nr^2/2},
\]
let \(P_t^n\) be the semigroup of the mollified equation obtained by replacing \(\delta_0\) with \(b_n\). For every \(t>0\), \(v\in E\), and \(f\in C_b(E)\),
\[
P_t^n f(v)\longrightarrow P_tf(v),
\]
equivalently \(P_t^n(v,\cdot)\Rightarrow P_t(v,\cdot)\).
- Define
\[
F_n(r):=\int_{-\infty}^r b_n(s)\,ds,\qquad
\Phi_n(v):=\int_0^1F_n(v(x))\,dx,\qquad
\Phi(v):=\int_0^1\mathbf 1_{\{v(x)>0\}}\,dx.
\]
- Set
\[
\nu_n(dv):=Z_n^{-1}e^{2\Phi_n(v)}\gamma(dv),
\qquad
\nu(dv):=Z^{-1}e^{2\Phi(v)}\gamma(dv),
\]
where \(Z_n\) and \(Z\) are normalizing constants. Since \(0\le\Phi_n,\Phi\le1\),
\[
1\le Z_n,Z\le e^2,
\qquad
e^{-2}\le \frac{d\nu_n}{d\gamma},\frac{d\nu}{d\gamma}\le e^2.
\]
- A probability measure \(\mu\) is invariant for a semigroup \((P_t)\) if \(\mu P_t=\mu\) for all \(t\ge0\).

Statement:
The target assertion from the original problem is
\[
\tag{U}
\text{\((P_t)\) has at most one invariant probability measure on \(E\).}
\]
Under the ABLM-type assumption above, the Gibbs measure \(\nu\) is invariant. Moreover, \((U)\) follows if one adds any of the deterministic-start smoothing or overlap hypotheses isolated below: compact-local Hellinger overlap, compact-local uniform \(\nu\)-absolute continuity together with uniqueness among \(\nu\)-absolutely continuous invariant measures, or pointwise resolvent absolute continuity together with uniqueness among \(\nu\)-absolutely continuous invariant measures. In that case the unique invariant probability measure is \(\nu\).
</THEOREM_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="2">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(E\) be Polish and \(K\) a Markov kernel on \(E\).
- Assume
\[
K(x,\cdot)\not\perp K(y,\cdot)\qquad\forall x,y\in E.
\]

Statement:
The kernel \(K\) has at most one invariant probability measure. Consequently, if a semigroup \((P_t)\) has some \(t_0>0\) such that
\[
P_{t_0}(x,\cdot)\not\perp P_{t_0}(y,\cdot)
\qquad\forall x,y\in E,
\]
then \((P_t)\) has at most one invariant probability measure.
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="2.1">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(K\) be a Markov kernel on \(E\).
- Let \(\eta\) be a finite signed measure.

Statement:
\[
|\eta K|\le |\eta|K.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="2.1">
For Borel \(A\), take the supremum over finite measurable partitions \(\{A_i\}\) of \(A\):
\[
|\eta K|(A)
\le
\sup_{\{A_i\}}\sum_i\int_EK(x,A_i)|\eta|(dx)
=
\int_EK(x,A)|\eta|(dx).
\]
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="2.1"></DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="2.2">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(K\) be a Markov kernel.
- Let \(\mu_1\ne\mu_2\) be invariant probability measures for \(K\).
- Let \(\sigma:=\mu_1-\mu_2\) and write its Jordan decomposition as \(\sigma=\sigma^+-\sigma^-\).

Statement:
If \(m:=\sigma^+(E)=\sigma^-(E)>0\), then
\[
\alpha:=\sigma^+/m,\qquad \beta:=\sigma^-/m
\]
are invariant probabilities and \(\alpha\perp\beta\).
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="2.2">
Since \(\sigma K=\sigma\), Lemma 2.1 gives \(|\sigma|\le|\sigma|K\). The two measures have equal total mass, so \(|\sigma|K=|\sigma|\). Hence
\[
\sigma^+K=\frac12(|\sigma|K+\sigma K)=\sigma^+,
\qquad
\sigma^-K=\frac12(|\sigma|K-\sigma K)=\sigma^-.
\]
Normalizing by \(m\) gives invariant probabilities. Their singularity follows from the Jordan decomposition.
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="2.3">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(K\) be a Markov kernel.
- Let \(\alpha,\beta\) be invariant probabilities for \(K\) with \(\alpha\perp\beta\).

Statement:
There exist \(x,y\in E\) such that
\[
K(x,\cdot)\perp K(y,\cdot).
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="2.3">
Choose a Borel set \(A\) with \(\alpha(A)=1\) and \(\beta(A)=0\). Invariance gives
\[
1=\int_EK(x,A)\alpha(dx),
\qquad
0=\int_EK(y,A)\beta(dy).
\]
Therefore \(K(x,A)=1\) for \(\alpha\)-a.e. \(x\), and \(K(y,A)=0\) for \(\beta\)-a.e. \(y\). Choosing such \(x,y\), \(K(x,\cdot)\) is concentrated on \(A\) while \(K(y,\cdot)\) is concentrated on \(A^c\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="2.3"></DEPS>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="2">
If two distinct invariant probabilities existed, Lemma 2.2 would produce singular invariant probabilities \(\alpha,\beta\). Lemma 2.3 would then produce \(x,y\) with \(K(x,\cdot)\perp K(y,\cdot)\), contradicting the pairwise non-singularity assumption. The semigroup assertion follows because every invariant measure for \((P_t)\) is invariant for the skeleton \(P_{t_0}\).
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="3">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- For probability measures \(\mu,\rho\) on \(E\), define
\[
\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)
:=
\int_E\sqrt{\frac{d\mu}{d\lambda}\frac{d\rho}{d\lambda}}\,d\lambda,
\]
where \(\lambda\) is any finite measure dominating both \(\mu\) and \(\rho\).
- Use the normalized total variation norm
\[
\|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}:=\sup_{A\in\mathcal B(E)}|\mu(A)-\rho(A)|.
\]

Statement:
For probability measures \(\mu,\rho\),
\[
\mu\perp\rho
\iff
\|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=1
\iff
\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)=0,
\]
and
\[
\|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le
\sqrt{1-\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)^2}.
\]
Moreover, if \(\mathcal P\) ranges over finite measurable partitions of \(E\), then
\[
\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)
=
\inf_{\mathcal P}\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}\sqrt{\mu(B)\rho(B)}.
\]
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="3.1">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(\lambda=\mu+\rho\), \(f=d\mu/d\lambda\), and \(g=d\rho/d\lambda\).

Statement:
The value of \(\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)\) is independent of \(\lambda\), and
\[
\mu\perp\rho
\iff
\|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=1
\iff
\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)=0.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="3.1">
Changing the dominating measure multiplies both densities by the same Radon--Nikodym factor, so the integral defining affinity is unchanged. If \(\mu\perp\rho\), then \(fg=0\) \(\lambda\)-a.e., hence \(\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)=0\). Conversely, if \(\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)=0\), then \(\sqrt{fg}=0\) \(\lambda\)-a.e.; the sets \(\{f>0\}\) and \(\{g>0\}\) separate \(\mu\) and \(\rho\). The equivalence with \(\|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=1\) is the standard characterization of singularity for probability measures.
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="3.1"></DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="3.2">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(\lambda=\mu+\rho\), \(f=d\mu/d\lambda\), and \(g=d\rho/d\lambda\).

Statement:
\[
\|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le
\sqrt{1-\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)^2}.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="3.2">
Compute
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
&=\frac12\int_E|f-g|\,d\lambda\\
&=\frac12\int_E|\sqrt f-\sqrt g|(\sqrt f+\sqrt g)\,d\lambda\\
&\le \frac12
\left(\int_E(\sqrt f-\sqrt g)^2\,d\lambda\right)^{1/2}
\left(\int_E(\sqrt f+\sqrt g)^2\,d\lambda\right)^{1/2}\\
&=\sqrt{1-\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="3.2"></DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="3.3">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(E\) be Polish.
- Let \(\mathcal P\) range over finite measurable partitions of \(E\).

Statement:
\[
\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)
=
\inf_{\mathcal P}\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}\sqrt{\mu(B)\rho(B)}.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="3.3">
For a finite partition \(\mathcal P\), on each atom \(B\),
\[
\int_B\sqrt{\mathbb E_\lambda(f\mid\mathcal P)\mathbb E_\lambda(g\mid\mathcal P)}\,d\lambda
=
\sqrt{\mu(B)\rho(B)}.
\]
Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\mathbb E_\lambda(\sqrt{fg}\mid\mathcal P)
\le
\sqrt{\mathbb E_\lambda(f\mid\mathcal P)\mathbb E_\lambda(g\mid\mathcal P)},
\]
so every partition sum is at least \(\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)\). Since \(E\) is Polish, \(\mathcal B(E)\) is countably generated. Choosing finite partitions increasing to a countable generating algebra, the bounded martingale convergence theorem, applied with \(\lambda=\mu+\rho\) so \(0\le f,g\le1\), gives partition sums converging down to \(\int\sqrt{fg}\,d\lambda\). Hence the infimum equals the affinity.
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="3.3"></DEPS>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="3">
Lemma 3.1 gives independence of the dominating measure and the singularity equivalences. Lemma 3.2 gives the total variation bound. Lemma 3.3 gives the partition formula.
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="4">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(K\) be a Markov kernel on \(E\).
- Suppose \(C\subset E\) is measurable and there is \(a>0\) such that
\[
\operatorname{Aff}(K(v,\cdot),K(w,\cdot))\ge a
\qquad\forall v,w\in C.
\]

Statement:
For all probability measures \(\alpha,\beta\),
\[
\operatorname{Aff}(\alpha K,\beta K)\ge a\,\alpha(C)\beta(C).
\]
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="4.1">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Work under the assumptions of Proposition 4.
- Let \(\mathcal P\) be a finite measurable partition of \(E\).

Statement:
\[
\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}\sqrt{(\alpha K)(B)(\beta K)(B)}
\ge
a\,\alpha(C)\beta(C).
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="4.1">
For each atom \(B\),
\[
\sqrt{(\alpha K)(B)(\beta K)(B)}
\ge
\int_C\int_C\sqrt{K(v,B)K(w,B)}\,\alpha(dv)\beta(dw),
\]
by Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the functions \(\sqrt{K(v,B)}\) and \(\sqrt{K(w,B)}\). Summing over \(B\in\mathcal P\) gives
\[
\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}\sqrt{(\alpha K)(B)(\beta K)(B)}
\ge
\int_C\int_C
\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}\sqrt{K(v,B)K(w,B)}

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="7">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(E=C_0([0,1])\), and let \(\nu\) be the Gibbs measure from Theorem 1.
- Fix \(t_0>0\).
- Assume \(\nu\) is invariant for \((P_t)\).
- Assume that for every \(x\in E\) and \(f\in C_b(E)\),
\[
P_{t_0}^nf(x)\to P_{t_0}f(x).
\]
- Assume that for every compact \(K\subset E\), the family
\[
\{P_{t_0}^n(x,\cdot): n\ge1,\ x\in K\}
\]
is uniformly absolutely continuous with respect to \(\nu\).
- Assume every \(P_t\)-invariant probability measure that is absolutely continuous with respect to \(\nu\) equals \(\nu\).

Statement:
\(\nu\) is the unique invariant probability measure of \((P_t)\).
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="7.1">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(\mu\) be invariant for \((P_t)\).
- Set
\[
\rho_n:=\mu P_{t_0}^n.
\]

Statement:
\[
\rho_n\Rightarrow\mu.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="7.1">
For \(f\in C_b(E)\), dominated convergence and the pointwise convergence hypothesis give
\[
\int_Ef\,d\rho_n
=
\int_EP_{t_0}^nf(x)\mu(dx)
\longrightarrow
\int_EP_{t_0}f(x)\mu(dx)
=
\int_Ef\,d\mu,
\]
where the last equality uses invariance of \(\mu\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="7.1"></DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="7.2">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(\mu\) be invariant for \((P_t)\).
- Assume the compact-local uniform absolute continuity hypothesis in Proposition 7.

Statement:
\[
\mu\ll\nu.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="7.2">
Let \(B\) be Borel with \(\nu(B)=0\). Fix \(\varepsilon,\eta>0\). By tightness of \(\mu\), choose compact \(K\) with \(\mu(K^c)<\eta\). By uniform absolute continuity on \(K\), choose \(\delta>0\) such that
\[
\nu(A)<\delta
\implies
\sup_{n\ge1}\sup_{x\in K}P_{t_0}^n(x,A)<\varepsilon.
\]
By outer regularity, choose open \(O\supset B\) with \(\nu(O)<\delta\). Then for every \(n\),
\[
\rho_n(O)
=
\int_EP_{t_0}^n(x,O)\mu(dx)
\le
\varepsilon+\eta.
\]
Since \(O\) is open and \(\rho_n\Rightarrow\mu\) by Lemma 7.1, Portmanteau gives
\[
\mu(O)\le\liminf_n\rho_n(O)\le\varepsilon+\eta.
\]
Thus \(\mu(B)\le\varepsilon+\eta\). Letting \(\varepsilon,\eta\downarrow0\) gives \(\mu\ll\nu\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<THEOREM_PROOF id="1">
Proposition 1 proves the invariance of the Gibbs measure \(\nu\). Under the compact-local Hellinger overlap assumptions, Proposition 6 gives pairwise non-singularity of a transition skeleton and hence uniqueness. Under the compact-local uniform absolute-continuity assumptions, Proposition 7 gives uniqueness once uniqueness among \(\nu\)-absolutely continuous invariant measures is available; Proposition 9 supplies this latter condition in the symmetric Gibbs Dirichlet-form setting. Under pointwise resolvent absolute continuity, Proposition 8 gives the same uniqueness conclusion, again with Proposition 9 as a sufficient source for uniqueness among \(\nu\)-absolutely continuous invariant measures. Since Proposition 1 supplies an invariant measure, any at-most-one conclusion identifies the invariant law with \(\nu\).
</THEOREM_PROOF>

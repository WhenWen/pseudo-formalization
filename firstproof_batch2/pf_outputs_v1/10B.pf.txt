<THEOREM_STATEMENT id="1">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \((M,\tau)\) be a separable tracial von Neumann algebra, let \(H=L^2(M,\tau)\), and let \(J\) be the modular conjugation, so that \(\rho(x)=Jx^*J\) denotes the right action of \(M\) on \(H\).
- Assume \(M\) is placed in the DKEP properly proximal framework: there is a unital von Neumann algebra \(\mathcal N\), called the normal-bidual compactification, containing the left copy of \(M\) and containing support projections for DKEP boundary pieces.
- A DKEP boundary piece \(X\subset B(H)\) means a hereditary \(C^*\)-subalgebra, invariant under the left and right \(M\)-actions, whose elements are treated as \(X\)-compact operators. Its DKEP support projection \(q_X\in\mathcal N\) is the projection obtained as the weak-* limit in \(\mathcal N\) of any contractive approximate identity of \(X\), equivalently the least projection supporting the weak-* closure of \(X\). The compact support \(q_K\in\mathcal N\) is the support projection of \(K(H)\).
- Let \(n\ge 1\), let \(U_1,\dots,U_n\) be sets with finite-cover condition
\[
\bigcap_{i=1}^n U_i=\varnothing,
\]
and let
\[
\mathcal F=\left\{\bigcap_{i\in I}U_i:\varnothing\ne I\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\right\}\cup\{\varnothing\}.
\]
- For every \(U\in\mathcal F\), suppose there is a DKEP boundary piece \(X_U\) with support projection \(q_U\in\mathcal N\). Assume \(q_{\varnothing}=q_K\). Assume also the support sandwich inequality
\[
q_Uq_Wq_U\le q_{U\cap W}
\]
in the positive order of \(\mathcal N\), for all \(U,W\in\mathcal F\).
- For a boundary piece \(X\), define
\[
S_X(M)=\{T\in B(H):[T,\rho(x)]\in X\text{ for every }x\in M\}.
\]
A state \(\psi\) on \(S_X(M)\) is \(M\)-central if \(\psi(xT)=\psi(Tx)\) for all \(x\in M\), \(T\in S_X(M)\). The algebra \(M\) is properly proximal relative to \(X\) if there is no \(M\)-central state on \(S_X(M)\) whose restriction to the left copy of \(M\) is normal. The algebra \(M\) has no amenable direct summand if there is no nonzero central projection \(z\in Z(M)\) such that \(zM\) is amenable.
- Use the following DKEP obstruction theorem as a black box: if \(M\) has no amenable direct summand and is not properly proximal, then there exists an \(M\)-central obstruction state \(\Omega\) on \(\mathcal N\), normal on \(M\), such that \(\Omega(q_K)=0\); moreover, if \(M\) is properly proximal relative to \(X_U\), then every such obstruction state satisfies \(\Omega(q_U)=1\).
- Assume \(M\) has no amenable direct summand and is properly proximal relative to each \(X_{U_i}\).

Statement :
Under these assumptions, \(M\) is properly proximal.
</THEOREM_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="1">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(A\) be a unital \(C^*\)-algebra.
- Let \(\phi\) be a state on \(A\).
- Let \(p\in A\) be a projection with \(\phi(p)=1\).

Statement :
For every \(a\in A\),
\[
\phi(a)=\phi(pap).
\]
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="1.1">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(A\), \(\phi\), and \(p\) be as in Proposition 1.
- Set \(e=1-p\).

Statement :
For every \(a\in A\),
\[
\phi(ea)=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\phi(ae)=0.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="1.1">
Then \(e\) is a projection and
\[
\phi(e)=\phi(1)-\phi(p)=0.
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz for the positive functional \(\phi\),
\[
|\phi(ea)|^2\le \phi(e^*e)\phi(a^*a)=\phi(e)\phi(a^*a)=0,
\]
so \(\phi(ea)=0\) for all \(a\). Similarly,
\[
\phi(ae)=\overline{\phi(ea^*)}=0.
\]

External results used: Cauchy--Schwarz inequality for positive functionals.
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="1.2">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(A\), \(\phi\), \(p\), and \(e=1-p\) be as in Proposition 1.

Statement :
For every \(a\in A\),
\[
a=pap+pae+eap+eae.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="1.2">
Since \(p+e=1\),
\[
a=(p+e)a(p+e)=pap+pae+eap+eae.
\]
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="1.2"></DEPS>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="1">
Apply \(\phi\) to the decomposition in Lemma 1.2. By Lemma 1.1, all terms except \(pap\) vanish: \(\phi(pae)=0\), \(\phi(eap)=0\), and \(\phi(eae)=0\). Hence \(\phi(a)=\phi(pap)\).
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="2">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- All products and inequalities are taken inside the normal-bidual compactification \(\mathcal N\).
- Let \(\phi\) be a state on \(\mathcal N\).
- Let \(U,W\in\mathcal F\).
- Assume
\[
\phi(q_U)=1,\qquad \phi(q_W)=1.
\]
- Assume the support sandwich inequality
\[
q_Uq_Wq_U\le q_{U\cap W}.
\]

Statement :
\[
\phi(q_{U\cap W})=1.
\]
No commutativity of the projections \(q_U\) and \(q_W\) is assumed.
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="2.1">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(\phi\), \(U\), and \(W\) be as in Proposition 2.
- Set \(p=q_U\) and \(s=q_W\).

Statement :
\[
\phi(q_Uq_Wq_U)=1.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="2.1">
By Proposition 1 applied to \(A=\mathcal N\), \(p=q_U\), and \(a=s=q_W\),
\[
\phi(s)=\phi(psp).
\]
Since \(\phi(s)=1\), this gives
\[
\phi(q_Uq_Wq_U)=1.
\]
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="2.2">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(\phi\), \(U\), and \(W\) be as in Proposition 2.
- Assume additionally
\[
\phi(q_Uq_Wq_U)=1.
\]
- Set \(r=q_{U\cap W}\).

Statement :
\[
\phi(r)=1.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="2.2">
By the support sandwich inequality,
\[
q_Uq_Wq_U\le q_{U\cap W}=r.
\]
Because \(r\) is a projection, \(0\le r\le 1\), so
\[
1=\phi(q_Uq_Wq_U)\le \phi(r)\le 1.
\]
Therefore \(\phi(r)=1\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="2.2"></DEPS>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="2">
Lemma 2.1 gives \(\phi(q_Uq_Wq_U)=1\). Lemma 2.2 then gives \(\phi(q_{U\cap W})=1\). No commutativity was used: \(q_Uq_Wq_U\) is positive because
\[
q_Uq_Wq_U=(q_Wq_U)^*(q_Wq_U),
\]
and the argument uses only the compression \(q_Uq_Wq_U\), not the product \(q_Uq_W\).
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>

<THEOREM_PROOF id="1">
Assume, toward contradiction, that \(M\) is not properly proximal. Proposition 3 gives an \(M\)-central obstruction state \(\Omega\) on \(\mathcal N\), normal on \(M\), such that
\[
\Omega(q_K)=0
\]
and
\[
\Omega(q_{U_i})=1
\qquad\text{for every }i=1,\dots,n.
\]
Proposition 4 applied to this state gives
\[
\Omega(q_K)=1.
\]
This contradicts the obstruction-state condition \(\Omega(q_K)=0\). Therefore the assumption that \(M\) is not properly proximal is false. Consequently, \(M\) is properly proximal.
</THEOREM_PROOF>

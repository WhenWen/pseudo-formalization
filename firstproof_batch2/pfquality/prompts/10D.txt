\psi(aT)=\varphi(e_AaTe_A)=\varphi(ae_ATe_A)=\varphi(e_ATe_Aa)=\psi(Ta).
\]
Thus \(\psi\) is strictly \(A\)-central.
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="4.1"></DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="4.2">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- The assumptions of Proposition 4 hold.
- \(\psi\) is defined as in Lemma 4.1.
Statement :
\(\psi\) extends the standard trace on \(A\).
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="4.2">
Since \(\varphi(e_A)=1\), we unconditionally have \(\varphi(1-e_A)=0\). The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for states ensures
\[
|\varphi(a(1-e_A))|^2\leq \varphi(aa^*)\varphi(1-e_A)=0.
\]
Hence, for \(a\in A\),
\[
\psi(a)=\varphi(e_Aae_A)=\varphi(ae_A)=\varphi(a)=\tau_M(a)=\tau_A(a).
\]
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="4.2"></DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="4.3">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- The assumptions of Proposition 4 hold.
- \(\psi\) is defined as in Lemma 4.1.
- \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(A)\) is the norm-closed ideal in \(\mathbb B(L^2A)\) generated by operators \(Xe_D^AY\), where \(X,Y\in A\) and \(D\subset A\) is an amenable von Neumann subalgebra.
Statement :
\(\psi\) completely vanishes on \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(A)\).
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<CLAIM_STATEMENT id="4.3.1">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- The assumptions of Lemma 4.3 hold.
- \(X,Y\in A\), and \(D\subset A\) is an amenable von Neumann subalgebra.
Statement :
\[
\psi(Xe_D^AY)=\varphi(Xe_D^MY).
\]
</CLAIM_STATEMENT>

<CLAIM_PROOF id="4.3.1">
Under the spatial identification, \(e_D^A\) corresponds precisely to the restriction of \(e_D^M\), so on \(L^2M\) it acts strictly as
\[
e_D^Me_A=e_Ae_D^Me_A.
\]
For any generator \(Xe_D^AY\),
\[
\psi(Xe_D^AY)=\varphi(e_AXe_D^MYe_A).
\]
Since \(X,Y\in A\) and \(A\) is a von Neumann subalgebra, the orthogonal projection \(e_A\) strictly commutes with \(X\) and \(Y\), allowing us to rewrite the argument as
\[
Xe_Ae_D^Me_AY=Xe_D^MY.
\]
</CLAIM_PROOF>
<DEPS id="4.3.1"></DEPS>

<CLAIM_STATEMENT id="4.3.2">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- The assumptions of Lemma 4.3 hold.
- \(X,Y\in A\), and \(D\subset A\) is an amenable von Neumann subalgebra.
Statement :
\[
\psi(Xe_D^AY)=0.
\]
</CLAIM_STATEMENT>

<CLAIM_PROOF id="4.3.2">
Because \(D\subset A\subset M\) is an amenable subalgebra of \(M\) and \(X,Y\in A\subset M\), the operator \(Xe_D^MY\) inherently belongs to the proper proximal ideal \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(M)\) strictly by definition. By the founding contradiction assumption, \(\varphi\) vanishes entirely on \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(M)\), so \(\varphi(Xe_D^MY)=0\). Claim 4.3.1 gives the desired equality.
</CLAIM_PROOF>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="4.3">
Claim 4.3.2 shows that \(\psi\) vanishes on every generator \(Xe_D^AY\) of \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(A)\). By linearity and norm-continuity, \(\psi\) completely vanishes on all of \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(A)\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="4.4">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- The assumptions of Proposition 4 hold.
- \(\psi\) is the functional defined in Lemma 4.1.
Statement :
The existence of \(\psi\) contradicts proper proximality of \(A\).
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="4.4">
By Lemmas 4.1, 4.2, and 4.3, \(\psi\) is an \(A\)-central state on \(\mathbb B(L^2A)\) extending \(\tau_A\) that vanishes on \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(A)\). However, \(A\) is properly proximal, which prohibits the existence of exactly such a state.

External citation: Boutonnet, Ioana, and Peterson (2021), central-state characterization of proper proximality.
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="4.4">4.1, 4.2, 4.3</DEPS>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="4">
Lemmas 4.1, 4.2, and 4.3 construct from \(\varphi\) an \(A\)-central state \(\psi\) on \(\mathbb B(L^2A)\) extending \(\tau_A\) and vanishing on \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(A)\). Lemma 4.4 gives the resulting contradiction with proper proximality of \(A\). Therefore no such \(\varphi\) can exist.
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>
<DEPS id="4">4.1, 4.2, 4.3, 4.4</DEPS>

<THEOREM_PROOF id="1">
Proceed by mathematical induction on \(n=|\mathcal V(\Gamma)|\). The base case \(n=3\) is Proposition 1.

Assume the theorem holds for all irreducible graphs with \(k\) vertices, where \(3\leq k\leq n-1\), and let \(\Gamma\) be irreducible with \(n\geq 4\) vertices. Proposition 2 gives a non-cut vertex \(v\), an irreducible induced subgraph \(\Gamma'=\Gamma\setminus\{v\}\), a properly proximal algebra \(A=M_{\Gamma'}\), and an amalgamated free product decomposition
\[
M_\Gamma=A\ast_C B.
\]
Assume for contradiction that \(M_\Gamma\) is not properly proximal. By Boutonnet, Ioana, and Peterson, there exists an \(M_\Gamma\)-central state \(\varphi\) on \(\mathbb B(L^2M_\Gamma)\) extending \(\tau_{M_\Gamma}\) and vanishing on \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(M_\Gamma)\). Proposition 3 gives \(\varphi(e_A)=1\). Proposition 4 says that no such state \(\varphi\) can exist. This contradiction establishes that \(M_\Gamma\) is properly proximal.

External citation: Boutonnet, Ioana, and Peterson (2021), central-state characterization of proper proximality.
</THEOREM_PROOF>

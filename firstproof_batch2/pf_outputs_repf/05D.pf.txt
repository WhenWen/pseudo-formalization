<THEOREM_STATEMENT id="1">
Let \(C_0([0,1]) := \{f\in C([0,1],\mathbb{R}) : f(0)=f(1)=0\}\). Let \(u(x,t)\) be a real-valued probabilistically weak mild solution, in the sense described in “Well-posedness of stochastic heat equation with distributional drift and skew stochastic heat equation” by Siva Athreya, Oleg Butkovsky, Khoa L\^{e}, Leonid Mytnik (2024), of
\[
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,t)
= \frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}(x,t)
+ \delta_0(u(x,t))+\dot W(x,t),
\qquad x\in[0,1],\ t\ge 0,
\]
with initial condition \(u(x,0)=u_0(x)\in C_0([0,1])\) and Dirichlet boundary conditions \(u(0,t)=u(1,t)=0\).

Assume that the existence, uniqueness, and approximation results of [ABLM24] extend to the SPDE on \([0,1]\) with Dirichlet boundary conditions. In particular, assume that there exists a unique in law adapted mild solution on \(C([0,T],C_0([0,1]))\).

For
\[
G_{1/n}(x):=\sqrt{\frac{n}{2\pi}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{n x^2}{2}\right),
\]
let \(u^n\) be the solution to the approximated SPDE with drift \(G_{1/n}\) in place of \(\delta_0\). Assume that for every \(t>0\),
\[
u^n(t)\to u(t)
\]
weakly as \(n\to\infty\).

Let \(P_t\) be the Markov semigroup associated with \(u(t)\):
\[
P_t f(u_0)=\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(u(\cdot,t))\mid u(\cdot,0)=u_0(\cdot)\bigr],
\]
for bounded measurable \(f:C_0([0,1])\to\mathbb{R}\).

Statement:
\(P_t\) has at most one invariant probability measure \(\mu\) on \(C_0([0,1])\).
</THEOREM_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="1">
For each \(n\ge 1\), let \(P_t^n\) be the Markov semigroup of the approximated SPDE
\[
\frac{\partial u^n}{\partial t}
= \frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^2 u^n}{\partial x^2}
+ G_{1/n}(u^n)+\dot W
\]
with Dirichlet boundary conditions on \([0,1]\). Let \(P_0\) denote the law of the standard Brownian bridge on \(C_0([0,1])\). Define
\[
\Phi_n(y):=\int_0^y G_{1/n}(z)\,dz
\]
and
\[
\mu_n(du)=\rho_n(u)P_0(du)
=\frac{1}{Z_n}\exp\!\left(2\int_0^1\Phi_n(u(x))\,dx\right)P_0(du).
\]

Statement:
\(\mu_n\) is the unique invariant probability measure for \(P_t^n\).
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="1.1">
Consider the linear stochastic heat equation on \([0,1]\) with Dirichlet boundary conditions and with \(G_{1/n}\equiv 0\).

The linear stochastic heat equation has a unique invariant probability measure \(P_0\) on \(C_0([0,1])\), namely the law of the standard Brownian bridge.
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="2">

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="2.1">
Let
\[

\qquad
R(u):=\exp\!\left(\int_0^1\operatorname{sgn}(u(x))\,dx\right).
\]

Statement:
For \(P_0\)-almost every \(u\in C_0([0,1])\), \(R_n(u)\to R(u)\), and \(R_n(u)\le e\) for all \(n\).
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<CLAIM_STATEMENT id="2.1.1">
For all \(y\in\mathbb{R}\),
\[
|\Phi_n(y)|\le \frac12.
\]
For every \(y\ne 0\),
\[
\Phi_n(y)\to \frac12\operatorname{sgn}(y).
\]
</CLAIM_STATEMENT>

<CLAIM_PROOF id="2.1.1">
The function \(G_{1/n}\) is the centered one-dimensional heat kernel on \(\mathbb{R}\) with variance \(1/n\). Its total mass is \(1\), and \(\Phi_n(y)\) integrates this density from \(0\) to \(y\). As \(n\to\infty\), the mass concentrates at \(0\), giving the right half-mass for \(y>0\) and the negative left half-mass for \(y<0\).
</CLAIM_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="4">
Let \(\mu\) be the measure from Proposition 2 and let \(P_t\) be the Markov semigroup of the singular SPDE.

Statement:
\(\mu\) is the unique invariant probability measure for \(P_t\).
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="4.1">
Statement:
The measure \(\mu\) is absolutely continuous with respect to \(P_0\), its density is bounded and bounded away from zero, and \(\mu\) has full support on \(C_0([0,1])\).
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="4.1">
By Proposition 2,
\[
\frac{d\mu}{dP_0}(u)
=
\frac{1}{Z}\exp\!\left(\int_0^1\operatorname{sgn}(u(x))\,dx\right).
\]
The exponent is bounded, so the density is bounded and bounded away from zero. Therefore \(\mu\) has the same support as \(P_0\), namely full support on \(C_0([0,1])\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="4.1">2</DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="4.2">
Statement:
The transition semigroup \(P_t\) is topologically irreducible: for every \(t>0\), every \(u_0\in C_0([0,1])\), and every non-empty open set \(U\subset C_0([0,1])\),
\[
P_t(u_0,U)>0.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="4.2">
The SPDE is driven by additive space-time white noise \(\dot W\), which acts on all Fourier modes. The proof uses this as the reason for topological irreducibility.
</LEMMA_PROOF>

On the finite spatial interval \([0,1]\), the bounded perturbation of the gradient potential makes \(\mu\) the unique Gibbs measure for the system, and the system is reversible with respect to \(\mu\).
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

For \(f,g\in C_0([0,1])\), the path \(s\mapsto (1-s)f+sg\), \(s\in[0,1]\), lies in \(C_0([0,1])\) and connects \(f\) to \(g\).

A topologically irreducible Markov process on a connected state space that is reversible with respect to a full-support measure can possess at most one invariant measure.
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_PROOF id="4">
Proposition 3 gives that \(\mu\) is invariant. Lemma 4.1 gives full support, Lemma 4.2 gives topological irreducibility, Lemma 4.3 gives reversibility with respect to \(\mu\), and Lemma 4.4 gives connectedness of the state space. Applying Lemma 4.5 gives that there is at most one invariant probability measure. Since \(\mu\) is invariant, it is the unique invariant probability measure.
</PROPOSITION_PROOF>

<THEOREM_STATEMENT id="1">
Let
\[
E:=C_0([0,1])=\{v\in C([0,1],\mathbb R):v(0)=v(1)=0\},
\qquad H:=L^2(0,1),
\]
with \(E\) equipped with the supremum norm. Let
\[
e_k(x):=\sqrt2\sin(k\pi x),\qquad \lambda_k:=\pi^2k^2,\qquad k\ge1,
\]
be the Dirichlet eigenbasis of \(-\partial_{xx}\). Let \(\gamma\) denote Brownian-bridge law on \(E\), equivalently the centered Gaussian measure with covariance \((-
\partial_{xx})^{-1}\):
\[
\mathbb E_\gamma[(v,e_k)_H(v,e_\ell)_H]=\frac{\delta_{k\ell}}{\lambda_k}.
\]

Consider the Dirichlet stochastic heat equation
\[
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,t)
=
\frac12\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2}(x,t)
+\delta_0(u(x,t))+\dot W(x,t),
\qquad
u(x,0)=u_0(x)\in E,
\qquad
u(0,t)=u(1,t)=0.
\]
Assume the ABLM-type input on the bounded interval: for every \(u_0\in E\) and \(T>0\), there is a unique-in-law adapted mild solution \(u\in C([0,T],E)\), and the solutions define a Markov semigroup \((P_t)_{t\ge0}\) on \(E\). For
\[
b_n(r):=G_{1/n}(r)=\sqrt{\frac n{2\pi}}e^{-nr^2/2},
\]
let \(P_t^n\) be the semigroup of the mollified equation obtained by replacing \(\delta_0\) with \(b_n\), and assume that for every \(t>0\), \(v\in E\), and \(f\in C_b(E)\),
\[
P_t^n f(v)\longrightarrow P_tf(v),
\]
equivalently \(P_t^n(v,\cdot)\Rightarrow P_t(v,\cdot)\).

Define
\[
F_n(r):=\int_{-\infty}^r b_n(s)\,ds,\qquad
\Phi_n(v):=\int_0^1F_n(v(x))\,dx,\qquad
\Phi(v):=\int_0^1\mathbf 1_{\{v(x)>0\}}\,dx,
\]
and
\[
\nu_n(dv):=Z_n^{-1}e^{2\Phi_n(v)}\gamma(dv),
\qquad
\nu(dv):=Z^{-1}e^{2\Phi(v)}\gamma(dv),
\]
where \(Z_n\) and \(Z\) are the normalizing constants.

Under the ABLM-type input, the Gibbs measure
\[
\nu(dv)
=
Z^{-1}\exp\!\left(2\int_0^1\mathbf 1_{\{v(x)>0\}}\,dx\right)\gamma(dv)
\]
is invariant for \((P_t)\).

Moreover, the assertion
\[
\text{\((P_t)\) has at most one invariant probability measure on \(E\).}
\]
holds if one adds any one of the following deterministic-start smoothing or overlap hypotheses.

1. A compact-local Hellinger overlap hypothesis: for some \(0<\varepsilon<t\), the weak convergence \(P_t^n(x,\cdot)\Rightarrow P_t(x,\cdot)\) and \(P_{t-\varepsilon}^n(x,\cdot)\Rightarrow P_{t-\varepsilon}(x,\cdot)\) holds for every \(x\in E\), and for every compact \(C\subset E\) there is \(a(C)>0\) such that
\[
\operatorname{Aff}(P_\varepsilon^n(v,\cdot),P_\varepsilon^n(w,\cdot))\ge a(C)
\]
for all \(n\ge1\) and all \(v,w\in C\).

2. A compact-local uniform absolute-continuity hypothesis: for some \(t_0>0\), \(P_{t_0}^nf(x)\to P_{t_0}f(x)\) for every \(x\in E\) and \(f\in C_b(E)\), the family
\[
\{P_{t_0}^n(x,\cdot):n\ge1,\ x\in K\}
\]
is uniformly absolutely continuous with respect to \(\nu\) for every compact \(K\subset E\), and every \(P_t\)-invariant probability measure absolutely continuous with respect to \(\nu\) equals \(\nu\).

3. A pointwise resolvent absolute-continuity hypothesis: \(\nu\) is invariant, every invariant probability measure absolutely continuous with respect to \(\nu\) equals \(\nu\), and for some \(\lambda>0\),
\[
R_\lambda(x,\cdot):=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}P_t(x,\cdot)\,dt\ll\nu
\qquad\forall x\in E.
\]

The condition that every invariant probability measure absolutely continuous with respect to \(\nu\) equals \(\nu\) holds, for example, for a \(\nu\)-symmetric Markov semigroup whose closed Dirichlet form has only constant zero-energy functions. For the Gibbs gradient form
\[
\mathcal E(F,G)=\frac12\int_E\langle DF,DG\rangle_H\,d\nu,
\]
this zero-energy condition holds provided the limiting semigroup is the symmetric semigroup associated with this form.

In each conditional uniqueness case, the unique invariant probability measure is \(\nu\).
</THEOREM_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="2">
Let \(E\) be Polish and \(K\) a Markov kernel on \(E\). If
\[
K(x,\cdot)\not\perp K(y,\cdot)\qquad\forall x,y\in E,
\]
then \(K\) has at most one invariant probability measure.

If a semigroup \((P_t)\) has some \(t_0>0\) such that
\[
P_{t_0}(x,\cdot)\not\perp P_{t_0}(y,\cdot)\qquad\forall x,y\in E,
\]
then \((P_t)\) has at most one invariant probability measure.
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="2.1">
Let \(K\) be a Markov kernel on \(E\). If \(\mu_1\ne\mu_2\) are invariant for \(K\), \(\sigma:=\mu_1-\mu_2\), and \(\sigma=\sigma^+-\sigma^-\) is the Jordan decomposition, then
\[
m:=\sigma^+(E)=\sigma^-(E)>0
\]
and
\[
\alpha:=\sigma^+/m,\qquad \beta:=\sigma^-/m
\]
are invariant probability measures with \(\alpha\perp\beta\).
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<CLAIM_STATEMENT id="2.1.1">
For every finite signed measure \(\eta\),
\[
|\eta K|\le |\eta|K.
\]
</CLAIM_STATEMENT>

<CLAIM_PROOF id="2.1.1">
For Borel \(A\), take the supremum over finite measurable partitions \(\{A_i\}\) of \(A\):
\[
|\eta K|(A)
\le
\sup_{\{A_i\}}\sum_i\int_EK(x,A_i)|\eta|(dx)
=
\int_EK(x,A)|\eta|(dx).
\]
</CLAIM_PROOF>
<DEPS id="2.1.1"></DEPS>

<CLAIM_STATEMENT id="2.1.2">
If \(\sigma K=\sigma\) and \(\sigma(E)=0\), then
\[
|\sigma|K=|\sigma|
\]
and
\[
\sigma^+K=\sigma^+,\qquad \sigma^-K=\sigma^-.
\]
</CLAIM_STATEMENT>

<CLAIM_PROOF id="2.1.2">
The inequality for signed measures gives \(|\sigma|\le|\sigma|K\). The two measures have equal total mass, so equality holds. Therefore
\[
\sigma^+K=\frac12(|\sigma|K+\sigma K)=\sigma^+,
\qquad
\sigma^-K=\frac12(|\sigma|K-\sigma K)=\sigma^-.
\]
</CLAIM_PROOF>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="3">
Let \(\mu,\rho\) be probability measures on a measurable space \(E\). Define
\[
\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)
:=
\int_E\sqrt{\frac{d\mu}{d\lambda}\frac{d\rho}{d\lambda}}\,d\lambda,
\]
where \(\lambda\) is any finite measure dominating both \(\mu\) and \(\rho\). Use the normalized total variation norm
\[
\|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}:=\sup_{A\in\mathcal B(E)}|\mu(A)-\rho(A)|.
\]

\[
\mu\perp\rho
\iff
\|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=1
\iff
\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)=0,
\]
and
\[
\|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le
\sqrt{1-\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)^2}.
\]
If \(E\) is Polish and \(\mathcal P\) ranges over finite measurable partitions of \(E\), then
\[
\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)
=
\inf_{\mathcal P}\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}\sqrt{\mu(B)\rho(B)}.
\]
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="3.1">
The value of \(\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)\) is independent of the dominating measure \(\lambda\), and
\[
\mu\perp\rho\iff \operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)=0.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="3.1">
Changing the dominating measure multiplies both densities by the same Radon--Nikodym factor, leaving the integral unchanged. Taking \(\lambda=\mu+\rho\) and writing \(f=d\mu/d\lambda\), \(g=d\rho/d\lambda\), singularity gives \(fg=0\) \(\lambda\)-almost everywhere and hence \(\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)=0\). Conversely, if the affinity is \(0\), then \(\sqrt{fg}=0\) \(\lambda\)-almost everywhere, and the sets \(\{f>0\}\) and \(\{g>0\}\) separate \(\mu\) and \(\rho\).
</LEMMA_PROOF>
<DEPS id="3.1"></DEPS>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="3.2">
For probability measures \(\mu,\rho\),
\[
\mu\perp\rho\iff \|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=1,
\]
and
\[
\|\mu-\rho\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le
\sqrt{1-\operatorname{Aff}(\mu,\rho)^2}.
\]
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<CLAIM_STATEMENT id="3.2.1">

<THEOREM_PROOF id="1">
Proposition 1 proves invariance of the Gibbs measure \(\nu\) under the ABLM-type input.

Under the compact-local Hellinger overlap hypothesis, Proposition 6 gives pairwise non-singularity of a transition skeleton, and Proposition 2 gives uniqueness of invariant probability measures.

Under the compact-local uniform absolute-continuity hypothesis, Proposition 7 shows that every invariant probability measure is absolutely continuous with respect to \(\nu\) and hence equals \(\nu\), with Proposition 9 supplying the stated Dirichlet-form criterion for the absolute-continuity uniqueness hypothesis.

Under the pointwise resolvent absolute-continuity hypothesis, Proposition 8 gives the same uniqueness conclusion, again with Proposition 9 supplying the stated Dirichlet-form criterion when applicable.

</THEOREM_PROOF>

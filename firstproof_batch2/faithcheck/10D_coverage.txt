Overall, the PF preserves the main mathematical spine of the proof, but it is **not a complete faithful preservation** of the full author text. It sanitises notation, omits some explicitly stated definitions/explanatory facts, adds formal structure and citations, and sometimes turns author-defined constructions into proposition “assumptions.”

## Findings

### 1. The PF drops the document front matter and the separate “Problem Statement”

**Author text:**
> `\title{Submission D solution to Problem 10}`  
> `\date{}`  
> `\maketitle`  
> `\section*{Problem Statement}`  
> Prove the following statement:  
> Let \(\Gamma\) be ... then the graph product von Neumann algebra \(M\) is properly proximal.

**PF:**
> Starts directly with  
> `<THEOREM_STATEMENT id="1"> ...`

There is no title, date, maketitle, or separate problem-statement section.

**Why it matters:**  
If the audit is for whole-document coverage, the PF omits author-supplied non-proof framing and the original problem statement. The theorem statement in the PF mostly covers the mathematical content, but the original problem statement contained additional wording, notation, and an explicit definition of irreducibility that are not preserved verbatim.

### 2. The author’s explicit definition of irreducibility from the problem statement is omitted/sanitised

**Author text:**
> If \(\Gamma\) is irreducible  
> \[
> \text{(in the sense that there does not exists subgraphs } \GG_1,\GG_2
> \text{ such that } \VV(\GG_1)\cup \VV(\GG_2)=\VV(\GG)
> \text{ and every } (v,u)\in \EE(\GG)
> \text{ for all } v\in\VV(\GG_1),u\in \VV(\GG_2))
> \]
> ...

**PF:**
> \(\Gamma\) is irreducible, equivalently its complement graph \(\Gamma^c\) is connected.

**Why it matters:**  
The PF replaces the author’s explicit “no join decomposition” style definition by the cleaner equivalence “\(\Gamma^c\) is connected.” The author’s theorem later also says “irreducible (its complement graph \(\Gamma^c\) is connected),” so the PF preserves the theorem’s formulation, but it does **not** preserve the original problem-statement definition. This sanitises the author’s possibly imprecise/nonstandard wording and removes the explicit subgraphs \(\GG_1,\GG_2\) and edge condition.

### 3. The PF sanitises/renames the author’s notation

**Author text:**
> \(|\VV(\GG)|\geq 3\)  
> \(v\in \VV\)  
> \(\EE(\GG)\)  
> the graph product von Neumann algebra \(M\)

and later:

**PF:**
> \(|\mathcal V(\Gamma)|\geq 3\)  
> \(v\in \mathcal V(\Gamma)\)

**Why it matters:**  
The PF normalises the author’s notation: \(\VV\) becomes \(\mathcal V\), \(\GG\) is corrected to \(\Gamma\), and the problem-statement algebra \(M\) is standardised to \(M_\Gamma\). This is mathematically harmless, but it is a renaming/sanitisation relative to the original text.

### 4. The PF omits the author’s full definition of \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(M)\)

**Author text:**
> where \(\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{prop}}(M)\) is the norm-closed proper proximal ideal generated by  
> \[
> \{x e_D^M y \mid x, y \in M, D \subset M \text{ an amenable von Neumann subalgebra}\}.
> \]

**PF:**
In Proposition 3 the PF only assumes:
> \(\varphi\) is an \(M\)-central state ... vanishing on \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(M)\).

The PF later defines the analogous ideal for \(A\):

> \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(A)\) is the norm-closed ideal in \(\mathbb B(L^2A)\) generated by operators \(Xe_D^AY\), where \(X,Y\in A\) and \(D\subset A\) is an amenable von Neumann subalgebra.

But it never reproduces the author’s explicit definition of \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(M)\).

**Why it matters:**  
The proof later relies on the fact that \(Xe_D^M Y\in \mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(M)\). The author explicitly defined this ideal for \(M\); the PF uses \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(M)\) as if already known and only gives the generator definition for \(A\). This is a genuine omission of an author-stated definition.

In the theorem proof:

and later:
> External citation: Boutonnet, Ioana, and Peterson (2021), central-state characterization of proper proximality.

**Why it matters:**  
The PF does not preserve the author’s detailed statement of the criterion at the same point. It also labels the result as the “central-state characterization of proper proximality,” a name the author did not use. The mathematical use is compatible with the author’s argument, but the attribution and formulation are altered.

### 9. The PF drops the author’s explicit sentence relating \(N_G(v)\), \(L\), and \(C\)

**Author text:**

**PF:**
The PF defines:

Later it uses statements such as:

But there is no PF sentence corresponding to:

**Why it matters:**  
The PF preserves the needed consequence \(x,y\notin L\), but it drops the author’s explicit explanatory relation between \(N_G(v)\), non-adjacency in \(\Gamma\), and the algebra \(C=M_L\). In particular, the algebraic note \(M_{N_G(v)}\not\subset C\) is absent.

## Summary

The PF captures the main proof strategy and most mathematical assertions, but it is not a fully faithful whole-proof preservation. The most substantive issues are:

1. the explicit problem-statement definition of irreducibility is omitted/sanitised;
2. the definition of \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{prop}}(M)\) is not reproduced;
3. some author-stated graph-product orthogonality and \(N_G(v)\)/\(L\)/\(C\) relations are dropped or only used implicitly;
4. citations are relocated, duplicated, and in places added;
5. the PF adds a large formal lemma/proposition/dependency structure and some extra proof details not present in the author text.

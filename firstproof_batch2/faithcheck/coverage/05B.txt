The PF is **not** a faithful and complete whole-proof rendering. It preserves much of the main invariance/conditional-uniqueness machinery, but it drops several author-stated definitions, warnings, examples, remarks, citations, and makes a few alterations/additions.

## Findings

### 1. Author-stated notation for kernel pushforward and invariance is missing

**Author:**
> For a Markov kernel \(K\) and a probability measure \(\mu\), write  
> \[
> \mu K(A):=\int_EK(x,A)\mu(\dd x).
> \]
> A probability measure \(\mu\) is invariant for a semigroup \((P_t)\) if \(\mu P_t=\mu\) for all \(t\ge0\).

**PF:** No corresponding definition appears. The PF uses notation such as \(\nu_nP_t^n=\nu_n\), \(\rho_n:=\mu P_{t_0}^n\), and “invariant”, but does not define \(\mu K\) or invariance.

**Why it matters:** This is an explicitly stated piece of the author’s setup. The PF uses the notation as if already given, so the formalisation is incomplete at the level of definitions.

### 2. The PF omits the author’s explicit negative/scope statement about what is and is not proved under ABLM assumptions alone

**Author, Overview / Strategy:**
> The stated ABLM-type assumptions provide existence, uniqueness in law, and weak convergence of the mollified equations, but they do not themselves provide any of these deterministic-start smoothing or overlap estimates. Thus the unconditional conclusion proved here is invariance of \(\nu\), together with precise conditional criteria under which the desired uniqueness follows.

**Author, Final conclusion:**
> Under only the assumptions explicitly stated in the original problem---namely well-posedness and weak convergence of the mollified equations---the argument does not rule out invariant measures singular with respect to \(\nu\). Thus the candidate proof establishes invariance and conditional uniqueness, but an additional smoothing or overlap theorem is needed for an unconditional proof of \eqref{eq:target}.

**PF:** The PF says:
> Under the ABLM-type input, the Gibbs measure ... is invariant ... Moreover, the assertion ... holds if one adds any one of the following ...

but it does not include the explicit author conclusion that the ABLM assumptions alone **do not rule out singular invariant measures** and that the candidate proof is not an unconditional uniqueness proof.

**Why it matters:** This is a central interpretive conclusion of the original proof. The PF’s theorem-like packaging softens/omits the author’s explicit warning that the original target is not established unconditionally from the stated assumptions.

### 4. The PF omits the remark giving a simple sufficient condition for uniform absolute continuity

**Why it matters:** The author gives a concrete sufficient condition for one of the conditional uniqueness routes. The PF drops this useful criterion entirely.

### 5. The PF omits the example showing \(L^2(\nu)\)-irreducibility does not rule out singular invariant laws

**Author:**
> \begin{example}[Irreducibility in \(L^2(\nu)\) does not rule out singular invariant laws]
> Let \(E=[0,1]\), let \(\nu\) be Lebesgue measure, and define a Markov kernel
> \[
> K(x,\cdot)=
> \begin{cases}
> \nu(\cdot),&x\ne0,\\
> \delta_0(\cdot),&x=0.
> \end{cases}
> \]
> Then \(\nu K=\nu\) ... and \(\delta_0K=\delta_0\). On
> \(L^2(\nu)\), however, \(Kf=\int f\dd\nu\) \(\nu\)-a.e.; hence the \(L^2(\nu)\)

> \end{example}

**PF:** No corresponding example appears.

**Why it matters:** This example explains a limitation of the Dirichlet-form/zero-energy argument: it only controls invariant measures absolutely continuous with respect to \(\nu\), not singular ones. Dropping it removes an important warning from the author’s proof.

### 6. The PF omits the example showing weak convergence can destroy absolute continuity and overlap

**Author:**
> \begin{example}[Weak convergence can destroy absolute continuity and overlap]

> \mathcal N(0,1/n)\Rightarrow\delta_0,

> \end{example}

**PF:** No corresponding example appears.

**Why it matters:** This example directly supports the author’s claim that weak convergence of mollified kernels is insufficient for uniqueness. Its omission weakens the logical explanation of the gap.

### 9. The PF adds an incorrect “exactly \(L^1\)” statement for total variation convergence

**Author:**
> \[
> \int_E\left|
> Z_n^{-1}e^{2\Phi_n(v)}-Z^{-1}e^{2\Phi(v)}
> \right|\gamma(\dd v)\longrightarrow0.
> \]
> Hence \(\|\nu_n-\nu\|_{\TV}\to0\).

**PF:**
> The total-variation convergence is exactly the \(L^1(\gamma)\) convergence of the densities, with the normalized total variation convention used in the paper.

**Why it matters:** With the normalized total variation convention
\[
\|\mu-\rho\|_{\TV}=\sup_A|\mu(A)-\rho(A)|,
\]
one has
\[
\|\mu-\rho\|_{\TV}=\frac12\int |d\mu/d\gamma-d\rho/d\gamma|\,d\gamma,
\]
not “exactly” the \(L^1\)-norm. The convergence conclusion is still correct, but the PF adds a technically false equality not present in the author’s proof.

### 10. The PF weakens/alters the standalone conclusion of the compact-local uniform absolute-continuity theorem

**Author, Theorem \(\ref{thm:uac}\):**
> Then \(\nu\) is the unique invariant probability measure of \((P_t)\).

**PF, Proposition 7:**
> Every invariant probability measure of \((P_t)\) equals \(\nu\). If \(\nu\) is invariant, then \(\nu\) is the unique invariant probability measure of \((P_t)\).

**Why it matters:** The PF adds an explicit extra “If \(\nu\) is invariant” clause and changes the theorem into a conditional uniqueness/existence statement. In the whole author proof, \(\nu\)’s invariance was already established earlier, so this is logically repairable globally, but it is not a faithful rendering of the theorem as stated.

The PF is broadly aligned with the author’s counterexample argument, but it is not a perfectly faithful whole-proof rendering. ## Findings

### 1. The general problem statement/question is narrowed to the Fano counterexample

**Author text:**  
> Let \(M\) be a matroid, and let \(\Trop(M)\) be the corresponding tropical linear space...  
> Suppose \(M\) admits an order-reversing involution \(F\mapsto F^\perp\) on the lattice of flats \(\cLL(M)\).  
> ...  
> **Question.** Is there an order-reversing involution \(\Phi\) on \(\Dr(M)\) that extends the involution \(F\mapsto F^\perp\)?

**PF:**  
> Let \(M=F_7\) be the Fano matroid...  
> Statement: There is no order-reversing involution \(\Phi\) on \(\Dr(F_7)\) that extends the flat involution \(G\mapsto G^\perp\).

**Why it matters:**  
The PF formalizes the counterexample theorem directly, rather than preserving the full original problem framing as a general question about arbitrary \(M\). This is mathematically natural for the solution, but as a whole-proof preservation check, the original’s general setup and question are partly dropped/narrowed.

### 5. The PF adds proof details about atoms/coatoms not explicitly present in the author proof

**Author text:**  
> Since points are the atoms of \(\Dr(F_7)\) (covering only the empty set), any inclusion-reversing involution \(\Phi\) on \(\Dr(F_7)\) must map atoms to coatoms. As \(\Trop(F_7)\) has rank \(3\), the coatoms are precisely the rank \(2\) spaces.

**PF:**  
> Atoms cover only the empty set, and an inclusion-reversing involution sends covering relations at the bottom of the poset to covering relations at the top. As \(\Trop(F_7)\) has rank \(3\), the coatoms are precisely the rank \(2\) spaces. In rank \(3\), the elements covered only by the top element are the rank \(2\) tropical linear spaces.

**Why it matters:**  
The PF adds explanatory justification. This is not contrary to the author, but it is additional content not literally present.

## Overall assessment

The PF captures the main mathematical strategy and almost all central claims:

- use \(F_7\);
- choose a polarity sending \(\{1\}\) to \(L_1=\{1,2,3\}\);
- identify \(V_{\{1\}}\in\Dr_2\) with continuum-many contained points;
- identify \(V_{L_1}\in\Dr_1\);
- use order reversal to force a bijection between \(I(V_{\{1\}})\) and \(F(V_{L_1})\);
- classify the filter \(F(V_{L_1})\) as four elements;
- conclude \(4\neq\mathfrak c\).

However, it is not a perfectly complete or literal preservation: it narrows the original general problem framing to the counterexample theorem, softens/recasts some valuated-matroid quotient terminology, adds several clarifying arguments and exclusions, and drops the author’s explicit “known theoretical failure mode” discussion.

<THEOREM_STATEMENT id="1">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(M=F_7\) be the Fano matroid on \(E=\{1,\dots,7\}\).
- Let \(\Trop(M)\) be the closure of the Bergman fan of \(M\) in tropical projective space.
- Let \(\Dr(M)\) be the poset of tropical linear subspaces of \(\Trop(M)\), ordered by inclusion.
- Use the natural inclusion \(\cLL(M)^{\op}\subset \Dr(M)\) given by
\[
F\mapsto V_F:=\Trop(M/F\oplus U_{0,F}).
\]
- Let \(F=\{1\}\), and let the chosen standard polarity of the Fano plane send \(F\) to the line \(F^\perp=L_1=\{1,2,3\}\).

Statement :
There is no order-reversing involution \(\Phi\) on \(\Dr(F_7)\) that extends the flat involution \(G\mapsto G^\perp\).
</THEOREM_STATEMENT>

<PROPOSITION_STATEMENT id="1">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(M=F_7\) be the Fano matroid on \(E=\{1,\dots,7\}\).
- Let \(\Dr(F_7)\) be graded by rank.
- Let \(\Dr_1\) denote the rank \(1\) elements, i.e. the points of \(\Trop(F_7)\).
- Let \(\Dr_2\) denote the rank \(2\) elements, i.e. the tropical lines contained in \(\Trop(F_7)\).

Statement :
A standard polarity of the Fano plane induces an order-reversing involution \(G\mapsto G^\perp\) on \(\cLL(F_7)\), for instance sending \(\{1\}\) to \(L_1=\{1,2,3\}\). Moreover, any inclusion-reversing involution \(\Phi\) on \(\Dr(F_7)\) restricts to a bijection
\[
\Dr_1 \longleftrightarrow \Dr_2.
\]
</PROPOSITION_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="1.1">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(F_7\) be the Fano matroid, viewed as the matroid of the Fano plane.

Statement :
A standard polarity of the Fano plane induces an incidence-reversing bijection between its points, i.e. rank \(1\) flats, and its lines, i.e. rank \(2\) flats. Hence it yields an order-reversing involution \(G\mapsto G^\perp\) on \(\cLL(F_7)\). In particular, we may choose the polarity so that \(\{1\}^\perp=L_1=\{1,2,3\}\).
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="1.1">
A polarity of the Fano plane reverses incidence between points and lines, and this incidence reversal is precisely order reversal on the lattice of flats.
</LEMMA_PROOF>

<LEMMA_STATEMENT id="1.2">
Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- Let \(\Dr(F_7)\) be the poset of tropical linear subspaces of \(\Trop(F_7)\), ordered by inclusion.
- Let \(\Dr_1\) be the points of \(\Trop(F_7)\), and let \(\Dr_2\) be the tropical lines contained in \(\Trop(F_7)\).
- The tropical linear space \(\Trop(F_7)\) has rank \(3\).

Statement :
Since points are the atoms of \(\Dr(F_7)\), any inclusion-reversing involution \(\Phi\) on \(\Dr(F_7)\) maps atoms to coatoms. As \(\Trop(F_7)\) has rank \(3\), the coatoms are precisely the rank \(2\) spaces. Thus \(\Phi\) restricts to a bijection \(\Dr_1\leftrightarrow \Dr_2\).
</LEMMA_STATEMENT>

<LEMMA_PROOF id="1.2">
Atoms cover only the empty set, and an inclusion-reversing involution sends covering relations at the bottom of the poset to covering relations at the top. In rank \(3\), the elements covered only by the top element are the rank \(2\) tropical linear spaces.
</LEMMA_PROOF>

</PROPOSITION_PROOF>

<THEOREM_PROOF id="1">
Assume, for contradiction, that an order-reversing involution \(\Phi\) on \(\Dr(F_7)\) extends the flat involution \(G\mapsto G^\perp\). Proposition 3 implies that \(\Phi\) demands a bijection between the point ideal \(I(V_{\{1\}})\), of cardinality \(\mathfrak c\), and the filter \(F(V_{L_1})\), so \(|F(V_{L_1})|=\mathfrak c\). Proposition 5 gives \(|F(V_{L_1})|=4\). Since \(4\neq \mathfrak c\), no such order-reversing bijection can exist on the relative Dressian. Therefore, there is no order-reversing involution \(\Phi\) extending \(G\mapsto G^\perp\).
</THEOREM_PROOF>

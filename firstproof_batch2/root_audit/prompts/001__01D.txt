--- Theorem 1 ---
STATEMENT: Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.
- A representation \(\mathcal A\) of an infinite countable first-order structure has universe \(\mathbb N\). Its automorphism group is denoted \(\AUT(\mathcal A)\).
- \(\mathcal A\) is computably \(\AUT\)-countable on a cone if there is \(C\subset\mathbb N\) such that whenever \(\mathcal B\cong\mathcal A\), every automorphism of \(\mathcal B\) is computable relative to \(\mathcal B\oplus C\).
- A function \(f:\mathcal A\to\mathcal A\) is \(\Sigma^{in}_1\)-definable in \(\mathcal A\) relative to parameters \(\bar a=(a_1,\dots,a_n)\) if there is a family of existential formulas \((\phi_i)_{i\in\mathbb N}\) such that for all \(x,y\in\mathcal A\), \(f(x)=y\) iff for some \(i\in\mathbb N\), \(\mathcal A\models\phi_i(\bar a,x,y)\).

PROOF: Suppose, for contradiction, that such a countable computably-represented structure \(\mathcal A\) exists, and fix an oracle \(C\) witnessing computable \(\AUT\)-countability on a cone. Proposition 1 shows that \(\AUT(\mathcal A)\) is countable. Proposition 2 makes the identity isolated, and Proposition 3 gives a finite tuple \(\bar c\) with trivial pointwise stabilizer. --- Proposition 6 ---
STATEMENT: Assumptions / Conditions / Definitions.

Statement :

PROOF: Apply the Ash--Knight--Manasse--Slaman and Chisholm theorem: if \(M\) is a countable first-order structure, \(R\) is invariant under all automorphisms of \(M\), and there is an oracle \(C\subset\mathbb N\) such that for every structure \(\mathcal N\) with domain \(\mathbb N\) and every isomorphism \(f:M\to\mathcal N\), the relation \(f(R)\) is computable relative to \(\mathcal N\oplus C\), then \(R\) is definable in \(M\) by an \(L_{\omega_1\omega}\) \(\Sigma_1\) formula without parameters. Therefore \(R\) is definable in \(\mathcal A^*\) by a countable disjunction of parameter-free existential formulas.

- Let \(C\subset\mathbb N\) be such that whenever \(\mathcal B\cong\mathcal A\), every automorphism of \(\mathcal B\) is computable relative to \(\mathcal B\oplus C\).

Thus \(f(R)\) is computable relative to \(\mathcal N\oplus C\).
